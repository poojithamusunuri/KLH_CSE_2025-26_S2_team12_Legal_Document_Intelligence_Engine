All at: GF 01, Omaxe Park Plaza, S.K. ­II, Indrapuram, Ghaziabad, U.P. ........Accused Complaint under section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act CC No. 441/13 page no. 1 /16 Offence complained of : U/s 138 NI Act Date of commission of offence : 01.06.2013 Plea of Accused : Not guilty Final Arguments heard & Concluded on : 25.06.2014 Date of decision of the case : 05.07.2014 Final Order : Conviction BRIEF FACTS AND REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE: 1 Vide this Judgment I shall finally decide the complaint filed U/s 138 NI Act by Sh. Yogesh Giri, General Power of Attorney Holder of the complainant company Harsha Associates Pvt. Ltd. having its registered office at 117, Hargovind Enclave, Vikas Marg Extension, Delhi­92. The complainant's story in brief is that the complainant company and the accused company entered into an agreement dated 05.03.2013, whereby it was agreed that the accused company shall buy Shop no. G­36 in Harsha City Mall, Shakti Khand ­II, Plot B, Indirapuram, Ghaziabad (U.P.) for consideration of Rs.33,00,000/­ only. It is admitted by the complainant company that payment of Rs.17,15,000/­ has already been made by the accused company till 05.03.2013 and the accused company then issued cheque dated 10.04.2013 for Rs.5,00,000/­ as next installment of the payment. The said cheque was signed by accused no.2 and accused no.3, directors of the accused company. The said cheque was presented by the complainant company which was returned CC No. 441/13 page no. 2 /16 vide return memo dated 18.04.2013 with endorsement of "funds insufficient". Thereafter the complainant company issued legal notice dated 14.05.2013 which was sent on 14.05.2013. The complainant company has also stated that despite service of the legal notice the accused persons did not make any payment in discharge of their liability. 2 After the filing of the complaint and closing of pre summoning evidence, vide order dated 04.07.2013, summons were issued to all accused persons for the offence U/s 138 NI Act. It is noticed from the record inadvertently, due to typographical error, the pre summoning evidence though recorded on 04.07.2013 shows the date below the mentioning of CC No. as 03.07.2013. Further summoning order dated 04.07.2013 also contains typographical error whereby date below the signatures of the Ld. Predecessor is shown as 03.07.2013. It is clarified that pre summoning evidence was lead and closed on 04.07.2013 and also summons were issued to the accused persons vide order dated 04.07.2013. The accused persons were admitted to bail on 03.08.2013 and notice U/s 251 Cr. PC were also framed against the accused persons on 12.08.2013. The accused persons pleaded not guilty and claimed trial. Thereafter vide order dated 09.09.2013 the application U/s 145(2) NI Act was allowed and permission was granted to cross examine the complainant. 3 In order to prove its case the complainant examined Sh. Yogesh Giri, Authorized representative of the complainant company as CW­1 who reiterated the contents of the complaint on Oath and filed evidence by way of affidavit. CW­1 exhibited copy of General Power of Attorney and Board resolution as Ex. CW1/A (OSR) & Ex. CW1/B (OSR) respectively. He also exhibited the agreement dated 05.03.2013 as Ex. CW1/C, cheque dated 10.04.2013 bearing no. 154028 as Ex. CC No. 441/13 page no. 3 /16 CW1/D, return memo dated 18.04.2013 as Ex. CW1/E, legal notice dated 14.05.2013 as Ex. CW1/F, Speed post receipts dated 14.05.2013 as Ex. CW1/G, Ex. CW1/H & Ex. CW1/I and returned speed post envelops as Ex. CW1/J & Ex. CW1/K. Thereafter CW­1 was cross examined and discharged on 09.12.2013. 4 The complainant then examined Sh. Mahesh Kumar, Accountant in the complainant company as CW­2 who filed his evidence by way of affidavit which was exhibited as Ex. CW2/1. He stated in his evidence by way of affidavit that the accused persons had handedover four cheques of Rs.5,00,000/­ each to the complainant company for remaining consideration of the Shop, out of which two cheques were dishonored. He further stated that the accused persons had paid Rs. 1,00,000/­ after dishonor of cheques towards other liabilities but not towards the liablity arising out of dishonored cheques. He also produced attested copy of ledger account of the complainant company as Ex. CW2/A. Thereafter CW­2 was cross examined and discharged. The complainant closed his evidence with discharge of CW­2. 5 After that on 31.03.2014 the accused persons were examined U/s 313 Cr. PC and their respective statements were duly recorded. All the incriminating evidence were put to the accused persons, to which the accused persons admitted the agreement dated 05.03.2013 (Ex. CW1/C), cheque dated 10.04.2013 (Ex. CW1/D) & receipt of legal notice dated 14.05.2013 (Ex. CW1/F). However, the accused persons denied their liability to pay the complainant company. The accused persons stated that the legal notice was received despite payment made to the complainant company on which the accused persons contacted the director of the complainant company who told them to ignore the legal notice and pay the sum of CC No. 441/13 page no. 4 /16 Rs.1,00,000/­ to him. On this assurance the accused persons stated that they paid Rs.1,00,000/­ to the complainant company by way of RTGS. The accused persons also stated that the complainant company had not obtained necessary documents like Fire NOC and completion certificate and therefore the Mall was not ready for possession. The accused persons also stated that as per the agreement they had purchased shop of 333.33 square feet but was offered a shop of only 200 square feet and there were other dues payable by the complainant company to the accused company as commission for booking of shops through the accused company. They stated that those dues have also not been cleared and despite that the complainant company had started raising bills for maintenance without handing over the possession of the shop. Further the complainant had already taken a sum of Rs. 1,00,000/­ from the accused persons. The accused persons also stated that they want to lead defence evidence. However, the defence evidence was closed without leading any evidence by statement recorded on 19.05.2014 and the matter was fixed for final arguments. 6 Final arguments were addressed and heard on behalf of both the parties. Ld. Counsel for the complainant has reiterated the averments made in the complaint. He has argued that the accused persons have failed to disclose any defence and that the Rs.1,00,000/­ paid to the complainant was not a part payment against the dishonored cheque but was paid on account of other liabilities, therefore all ingredients of U/s 138 NI Act stands fulfilled and the accused persons are liable to be convicted. 7 On the other hand the Counsel for the accused has argued that the present complaint should be dismissed as there is no legal liability upon the CC No. 441/13 page no. 5 /16 accused persons to pay the complainant company. It was argued that as per the agreement dated 05.03.2013, the complainant had to handover the possession of the shop and since no possession has been handed over till date therefore, there is no question of the accused persons being liable to pay the consideration under the agreement. It was also argued that the complainant company is still not in the position to handover possession of the shop as documents like Fire NOC and completion certificate have not yet been obtained, which fact has been admitted by CW­1 in his cross examination. Hence, there is not legal liability upon the accused persons to pay to the complainant company. 8 Counsel for accused also argued that the present complaint is liable to be dismissed as the same has been filed by Authorized Representative who does not have personal knowledge of the transaction in question and therefore is not competent to depose as complainant witness to the facts of the case. Counsel for the accused also filed written arguments on 25.06.2014 wherein reliance has been placed on various judgments of Hon'ble Supreme Court and other Hon'ble High Courts: a) Shankar Finance and Investments V/s State of Andhra Pradesh and Ors. (2008)8 SCC536 b) Janki V. Bhojwani V/s Indusind Bank and Ors. Civil Appeal No. 6790 of 2003 decided on 06.12.2004 by the Hon'ble Supreme Court. c) National Small industries Corporation Ltd. V/s State (NCT of Delhi) and Ors. 2007(1) JCC 69(Delhi). d) Narender Kumar V/s Harnam Singh Criminal Appeal No. 11 of 1996 decided on 25.06.1999 by the Hon'ble High Court of Himachal Pradesh. e) Shamhu Dutt Shastri V/s State of Rajasthan and Ors. (citation not provided). CC No. 441/13 page no. 6 /16 f) Saida V/s Civil Judge (SD) and Ors. (citation not provided). g) Ram Prasad V/s Hari Narayan and Ors. (citation not provided). 9 Arguments advanced by both the parties have been heard at length. I have also perused the judicial record. 10 For offence U/s 138 NI Act the necessary ingredients to be proved are: a) That the accused had a legal liability / debt towards the complainant. b) That the accused issued a cheque in discharge of the said legal liability / debt. c) That the accused was maintaining a bank account for the cheque issued by him. D) That the cheque was presented by the complainant within its validity period. e) That the cheque was dishonored on his presentation. f) That the complainant issued legal demand notice within 30 days from the date of dishonor of the cheque. g) That the accused failed to pay within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice. If the above ingredients are proved then an offence punishable U/s 138 NI Act is committed. 11 In the present case the accused persons have not disputed the issuance of cheque and have admitted their signatures on the same. It has also been accepted by the accused persons that they received legal notice from the complainant. It has been stated by the accused persons in their examination U/s 313 Cr. PC that they CC No. 441/13 page no. 7 /16 have replied to the legal notice of the complainant vide reply notice dated 15.06.2013, delivered to the complainant on 18.06.2013. Therefore the disputed questions remaining to be decided are (a) whether the cheque in question has been issued in discharge of legal liability / debt, (b) whether the accused persons made any payment within 15 days of receipt of legal notice from the complainant, ( c ) Whether the present complaint has been filed by duly authorized power of attorney holder and ( d) whether the power of attorney holder is competent to depose as witness in this complaint. 12 Firstly, the question of liability of the accused persons to pay the complainant company shall be dealt with. In the present case the complainant has averred that the cheque were issued against discharge of liability under agreement dated 05.03.2013. Since the cheque in question is bearing the name of the complainant company, therefore, presumption arises U/s 118­A & 139 NI Act that the cheque was issued in discharge of a legal liability. Therefore, the onus to rebut this presumption is on the accused persons. The accused persons have disputed their legal liability to make payment on the ground that the complainant company did not handover possession of the shop as per agreement dated 05.03.2013. 13 On perusal of the agreement dated 05.03.2013 it is seen that the entire consideration for the shop was to be paid before taking over possession of the shop. The accused persons have admittedly paid an advance amount of Rs.17,15,000/­ to the complainant as per the endorsement attached with agreement dated 05.03.2013. However, as per clause 12 of the said agreement the accused company had agreed to take over possession of the shop on the condition that all amounts due and payable by them upto the date of possession is paid. Bare reading of the said clause clearly CC No. 441/13 page no. 8 /16 establishes that as per the agreement between the parties, possession was to be handed over only after receiving the due and payable amount. As per the endorsement with the agreement dated 05.03.2013, the accused persons were liable to pay Rs.5,00,000/­ on 05.04.2013 and further Rs.5,00,000/­ on 10.04.2013. Admittedly the accused persons are businessmen and it is not believable that the accused persons did not ask for return of the cheque in question from the complainant in case of the complainant not handing over possession of the shop like reasonable and prudent businessmen. The accused persons have not even put this to complainant witnesses in their cross examination. In the present case, if the contention of the accused persons is to be believed then there should have been demand for return of the cheque in question. However, no such demand has been made or no such legal notice has been issued by the accused persons. Further, the accused persons did not even issue any stop payment instructions to the bank which could support the claim that the cheque in question was not issued against any legal liability. It also shows that the accused persons had no objection against presentation of the cheque in question for encashment. 14 It is also not stated anywhere in the agreement dated 05.03.2013 that the accused shall not make any further payment apart from the advance amount already paid till the complainant company completes the construction of the Mall or obtains documents like Fire NOC / completion certificate or is ready to handover possession of the shop. Therefore, the defense taken by the accused that they were not liable to pay any money to the complainant on account of the complainant not handing over possession of the shop is without any merit. 15 It is true that for offence U/s 138 NI Act the accused is not expected to CC No. 441/13 page no. 9 /16 prove his defense beyond reasonable doubt in order to rebut the statutory presumption. However, in the instant case the agreement dated 05.03.2013 is supporting the case of the complainant and no where does it say that the accused would be liable to make payment to the complainant company only after taking over possession of the shop. The accused persons were required to lead defense evidence in order to rebut the statutory presumption of the legal liability / debt arising in favour of the complainant, however, the accused persons chose not to lead any evidence and therefore in absence of the same, accused persons have failed to rebut the statutory presumption. Hence, it stands proved that the cheque in question was issued by the accused persons in discharge of legal liability / debt. 16 The second disputed question that arises for consideration is whether the accused persons had made any payment to the complainant within 15 days of receiving legal demand notice. In the notice framed U/s 251 Cr. PC, the accused persons have stated that they have paid the amount of Rs.1,00,000/­ by way of RTGS on 29.05.2013 after receiving the legal notice. The accused persons have also placed on record copy of accounts statement from 03.05.2013 to 13.06.2013 showing transfer of Rs.1,00,000/­ by way of RTGS dated 29.05.2013. In reply to application U/s 145(2) NI Act, the complainant has admitted the deposit of the said amount, however, the complainant has stated that the said Rs.1,00,000/­ was deposited against other dues owed by the accused persons and not against the cheque in question. 17 It is well settled law that the cause of action arises U/s 138 NI Act only when legal demand notice has been served upon the accused and that the accused failed to pay the cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of the legal notice. The CC No. 441/13 page no. 10 /16 language used in Section 138 (c) of NI Act clearly establishes that there has to be an actual payment of the entire cheque amount within 15 days by the accused, otherwise cause of action for filing complaint U/s 138 NI Act arises in favour of the complainant. Tendering part payment of cheque amount after receipt of notice, within 15 days, is not payment made within the meaning of Section 138 ( c) NI Act, hence, the accused can not evade prosecution even if the said part payment has been accepted by the complainant. The same has been held by the Hon'ble Calcutta High Court in the case of M/s Ancon Engineering Co. (P) Ltd v. Amitava Goswami 1994 Crl. LJ 351. Hence, the defence of part payment raised by the accused is actually no defense and thus rejected. 18 The main contention / defense raised by the accused persons is that the present complaint is not maintainable as it has been filed by Power of Attorney holder who has not filed original Power of Attorney. Further, the accused has stated that the power of attorney holder did not have personal knowledge regarding the facts of the case and the complainant who had personal knowledge has not deposed before this Court. Therefore, the evidence of power of attorney holder can not be considered and hence, the present complaint is liable to be dismissed. 19 Although the original power of attorney has not been placed on record yet it is seen from the pre summoning evidence dated 04.07.2013 that original of Ex. CW1/A (OSR) was shown to the Court before exhibiting the photocopy on record. Further, it is also noticed that original of Ex. CW1/B (OSR) which is the minutes of the meeting dated 10.09.2010 was shown to the Court before exhibiting the photocopy on record. Therefore, the objection that the original documents have not been placed on record is without merit as the photocopies have been exhibited only CC No. 441/13 page no. 11 /16 after perusal of the originals by the Court. 20 In the case relied upon by the accused persons namely Shanker Finance & Investment V/s State of Andhra Pradesh (2008) 8 SCC 536, the Hon'ble Apex Court had held that the attorney holder can not file the complaint in his own name as if he was the complainant, but he can initial criminal proceedings on behalf of the principal. Hence, Ex. CW1/A (OSR), which authorizes the present authorized representative to pursue both Civil and Criminal cases arising out of unpaid invoices / bills, is sufficiently granting required authorization to the present authorized representative to pursue the present case. 21 The accused persons have argued that from the evidence of attorney holder, it is clear that he is unaware of the transaction between the parties and therefore, could not have given evidence. The accused persons have relied upon the following portions of the attorney holder's cross­examination in support of their argument: "I do not know anything outside the terms of the complaint. ...... I am not aware if after issuance of allotment letter physical possession of the shop was given to the accused persons or not. ..... I have to check the records to state what was the total payment made by the accused persons. ...... Question : I put it to you that accused persons have already paid a sum of Rs. 28,15,000/­ in respect of the allotment ? CC No. 441/13 page no. 12 /16 Ans.: I do not know I can tell only after checking the records. Question : I put it to you that after dishonor of the cheque bearing no. 154027 and 154028 amount of Rs. 1,00,000 was transferred in the account of the complainant company by way of RTGS on 29.05.2013. i.e. within 15 days of receiving the legal notice? Ans. : I do not about the same. I have to check the records. It is not in my knowledge if the complainant has received the reply of legal notice. ...... Question : I put to you that accused persons were not liable to pay the cheque amount i.e. Rs. 10,00,000/­ to the complainant company on the date of the cheques? Ans. They have to pay as per agreement. I personally do not know if they were liable or not." 22 It is well settled that statutory presumption regarding liability of the accused to make payment exists in favour of the complainant and the onus to rebut the same is upon the accused. As already discussed above, the accused persons have failed to rebut this presumption of their liability to pay to the complainant the cheque amount. Further, ingredients of offence u/s 138 NI Act, like the signatures on cheque, issuance of cheque to the complainant, dishonour of cheque, receipt of legal demand notice and failure to make payment of cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice have been proved against the accused persons. The accused persons could have raised this defense of incompetence of attorney holder to give evidence in case they had successfully rebutted the statutory presumption of liability to pay. Only then, the onus would have shifted back to the complainant to prove the liability to pay and then it would have been detrimental to their case that CC No. 441/13 page no. 13 /16 the attorney holder is unaware about the transaction between the parties. However, in absence of this shifting of onus upon the complainant, all the necessary ingredients for offence under section 138, NI Act stand satisfied by the present attorney holder only. Further, although the attorney holder is neither a signatory nor a witness to the agreement dated 05.03.2013, pursuant to which the cheque in question had been issued, yet, the said agreement has not been disputed by the accused persons. Therefore, the said agreement, on account of admission, stands proved and there is no need for examination of person having personal knowledge about the said agreement on behalf of the complainant. It is also to be noted that the present argument under discussion is a technical one, and in absence of rebuttal of presumption, it does not deserve to be allowed as technical considerations should not defeat substantive justice. 23 The judgments relied upon by the accused are not relevant to the facts of the present case. Shanker Finance & Investment V/s State of Andhra Pradesh (2008) 8 SCC 536, is distinguishable as the same deals with a proprietorship concern, while the present complaint has been filed by a company. Janki V. Bhojwani V/s Indusind Bank and Ors. Civil Appeal No. 6790 of 2003 decided on 06.12.2004 is distinguishable as the same is concerned with civil law and not criminal proceedings. National Small Industries Corporation Ltd. V/s State (NCT of Delhi) and Ors. 2007(1) JCC 69(Delhi) deals with a complaint by government companies, while the present complaint has been filed by a private limited company. Narender Kumar V/s Harnam Singh Criminal Appeal No. 11 of 1996 decided on 25.06.1999 is not applicable to the facts of the present case because the cheque in question has admittedly been issued by the accused persons CC No. 441/13 page no. 14 /16 pursuant to the agreement dated 05.03.2013. Therefore, there is no question of the cheque not having been issued against legal liability. 24 There have been some miscellaneous arguments raised by the accused persons. One such argument raised by the accused persons is that the complainant has taken excess consideration than what was agreed in agreement dated 05.03.2013. The accused persons have submitted that the consideration as per agreement was Rs.34,50,000/­ while the complainant has already taken an amount of Rs.37,15,000/­ from the accused persons by way of cash and cheque. Therefore, the accused persons have argued that the present case is not maintainable. The accused persons have not led any evidence to prove their claim of having paid Rs. 37,15,000/­ to the complainant. The accused persons have only made bald statements in their application U/s 145(2) NI Act that the said amount had been paid. The said contentions had been reiterated in the alleged reply notice dated 15.06.2013. However, neither the said reply notice nor statement of account annexed with application U/s 145(2) NI Act have been proved by way of evidence by the accused persons. It is well established that statutory presumption U/s 138 NI Act exists in favour of the complainant and the onus of proving his innocence is upon the accused. Therefore the onus of proving that the accused persons have paid the agreed consideration or more is upon the accused persons. In absence of any defense evidence the same has not been proved and therefore this argument also does not merit any consideration. 25 The accused persons have further argued that the bona fides of the complainant is questionable as the complainant has deliberately suppressed the fact that they received reply notice dated 15.06.2013. It is well settled that the cause CC No. 441/13 page no. 15 /16 of action arises U/s 138 NI Act on failure of the accused to make payment within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice and it is immaterial that the accused replied to the legal demand notice without making payment of the cheque amount. Hence, the present argument is also without any merit. 26 Considering the entire evidence on record, it stands duly proved that the cheque in question Ex. CW­1/D was issued and drawn in discharge of legal liability of the accused persons and for consideration. 27 In view of the above discussion, I am of the considered opinion that the complainant has proved the ingredients for offence u/s 138 NI Act against the accused persons beyond reasonable doubt. Accordingly, accused M/s Janta Buildtech Pvt. Ltd. Alongwith its directors Sh. Ashu Gupta and Sh. Vipin Tyagi, GF­01, Omaxe Park Plaza, SK­II, Indirapuram, Ghaziabad (U.P.) stand convicted of the offence under section 138 NI Act.
