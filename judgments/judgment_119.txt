Bare perusal of Section 138 of the NI Act clarifies that five essential ingredients for completing the offence under Section 138 of the Act are as below: i. Drawing of the cheque, ii. Presentation of the cheque with the bank, iii. Returning of the cheque unpaid by the drawee bank, iv. Giving notice in writing to the drawer of the cheque demanding payment of the cheque amount, and v. failure of the drawer to make payment within 15 days of the receipt of the notice. 16. It is pertinent to mention here that there are two presumptions of law as mandated by the Negotiable Instruments Act. According to Section 118 (a) of the Act, it shall be presumed that every negotiable instrument was made or drawn for consideration. By virtue of this <span class="hidden_text" id="span_16">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 17 of 30</span> clause, the Court is obliged to presume that the promissory note was made for consideration or until the contrary is proved. In Mallavarapu Kasivisweswara Rao Vs. Thadikonda Ramulu Firm and Others AIR 2008 SC 2898 it was held as under: ­ "... ... ... The bare denial of the passing of the consideration apparently does not appear to be any defence. Something which is probable has to be brought on record for getting the benefit of shifting the onus of proving to the plaintiff. To disprove the presumption, defendant has to bring on record such facts and circumstances upon consideration of which the court may either believe that the consideration did not exist or its non­existence was so probable that a prudent man would, under the circumstances of the case, shall act upon the plea that it did not exist...." 17. According to Section 139 of NI Act "it shall be presumed, unless the contrary is proved that the holder of a cheque received the cheque for discharge in whole or in part, of any debt or other liability." Under Section 139 NI Act there is a legal presumption that the cheque was issued for discharging a liability and that presumption can be rebutted only by the person who drew the cheque. This presumption can be rebutted by the accused by adducing evidence. So the burden of proof is on the accused. 18. In Hiten P. Dalal Vs. Bratindranath Banerjee AIR 2010 SC <span class="hidden_text" id="span_17">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 18 of 30</span> 1898 it was held as under: ­ "The words 'unless the contrary is proved' which occur in this provision (Section 139) make it clear that the presumption has to be rebutted by 'proof' and not by a bare explanation which is merely plausible. A fact is said to be proved when its existence is directly established or when upon the material before it the Court finds its existence to be so probable that a reasonable man would act on the supposition that it exists. Unless, therefore, the explanation is supported by proof, the presumption created by the provision cannot be said to be rebutted......". 19. From the entire defence raised by Ld. So far as first contention of the accused is concerned that the cheques in question were lost and the same have been misused by the complainant is concerned, the same does not inspire confidence at all. It has been alleged by the accused that the cheques in question were lost on 01.04.2005. Legal demand notices were sent by the Ld. Keeping in view that he had lost so many <span class="hidden_text" id="span_19">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 20 of 30</span> cheques, duly signed by him, no reasonable man will be sitting idle waiting for the misuse of the cheques if he had lost so many blank signed cheques unless and until he knew as to with whom those cheques are lying. From the defence of the accused it appears that it is just an afterthought and a concocted story of loss of cheques. The defence of the accused is further weakened by the fact if we carefully peruse the cheques in the present complaints, it appears that the name and signatures were filled by the same pen. It shows that the name of the complainant and the signatures were put at the same time. Moreover, the accused had not denied that the cheques in question do not bear his signatures. Therefore, first contention of the accused is overruled. 21. Moreover the accused has also taken a defence that the cheques in questions were stolen cheques and on 01.04.2005 he had got a complaint registered with PS Karol Bagh to the effect and when lateron he came to know that the cheques were in possession of the complainant he had also lodged a case against the complainant in the year 2007 in Tis Hazari Courts. However the story of lost cheques does not inspire confidence at all. Because it is the accused who has <span class="hidden_text" id="span_20">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 21 of 30</span> to prove as to how the complainant succeeded to get possession of those cheques. The accused could have led defence evidence to prove that the cheques in question were stolen by the complainant. 22. So far as second contention of the accused is concerned that the complainant had no financial resource to pay such a heavy amount of Rs.17.5 lakhs is concerned, it can be said that the onus was upon the accused to prove the same. In fact, the complainant has produced copies of statement of account for the year 2003 to 2005 to show the withdrawals made by the complainant from the account of the firm Radhe Krishnan Impex and also from his brother's firm Paras Bajaj Fab. The statement of account furnished by the complainant clearly shows that several withdrawals were made by the complainant from his account which may not total upto Rs.17.5 lakhs but it certainly strengthens the case of the complainant and in view of the presumptions u/s 118 & 139 of N.I. Act the onus was upon the accused to show that these withdrawals were not meant for the purpose of partnership business. The complainant has failed to produce his ITRs of the relevant period but production of statement of account of the two firms mentioned above strengthens <span class="hidden_text" id="span_21">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 22 of 30</span> the case of the complainant to the effect that he had withdrawn amounts on various occasions from the account of the said two firms. Therefore second contention of the accused is also overruled. 23. So far as third contention of the accused that the complainant has not shown the alleged transactions in his ITRs is concerned, it can be said that merely because the complainant has not shown this amount in his ITRs, does not make the transactions illegal per se, as envisaged u/s 138 N.I. Act. As per Section 269 SS of Income Tax Act no person shall take or accept from any other person any loan or deposit otherwise than by an account payee cheque or account payee bank draft if.­ a) the amount of such loan or deposit or the aggregate amount of such loan and deposit is more than Rs.20,000/­ 24. Penalty for failure to comply with the provisions of Section 269 SS of Income Tax Act has been provided in Section 271 D of Income Tax Act. It provides that if a person take or accepts any loan or deposit in contravention of the provision of section 269 SS, he <span class="hidden_text" id="span_22">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 23 of 30</span> shall be liable to pay, by way of penalty, a sum equal to the amount of the loan or deposit so taken or accepted. 25. A bare reading of these sections shows that although loan taken or deposit of an amount more Rs.20,000/­ in cash is prohibited however in the considered opinion of this court if the penalty is imposed upon the defaulter and he pays the penalty imposed, the transaction is deemed to be legalized. Therefore if the person fails to comply with provisions of Section 269 SS of I.T. Act it can not be said that the transaction is altogether illegal in terms of Section 138 of N.I. Act and that the same can not be recovered from the person to whom it has been made. Therefore this contention of the accused that the transaction was not in compliance of Section 269 SS of Income Tax Act and the same does not fall within the purview of Section 138 of N.I. Act, also stands overruled. 26. Last contention of the accused was that the present complaints are liable to be dismissed on the ground that the same had not been pursued properly by the complainant and in fact the complainant has never appeared in the present complaints and POA holder has no personal knowledge to proceed with the present complaint and further that in the absence of personal knowledge, the POA holder <span class="hidden_text" id="span_23">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 24 of 30</span> can not depose on behalf of complainant and therefore the entire defence led by the complainant is liable to be ignored. To establish his arguments Ld. Counsel for the accused has relied upon the following judgments M/s Shankar Finance & Investments Vs. State of Andhra Pradesh & Ors. Cri Appeal No. 1449 of 2003 and A.C. Narayanan Vs. State of Maharashtra & Anr 2013 (11) Scale. 27. I have gone through both the judgments filed by Ld. Counsel for the accused and the observations made by Hon'ble Supreme Court are not in dispute. In fact it is not understandable as to how these judgments helped the case of the accused. In both the judgments referred by Ld. Counsel for the accused, it has been very specifically observed by Hon'ble Suprme Court as under: "Where the proprietor of the proprietary concern has personal knowledge of the transaction and the proprietor has signed the complaint, he has to be examined under S.200 of the Code. But where the Attorney holder of the complainant is in charge of teh business of the payee­complainant and the Attorney holder alone is personally aware of the transactions, and the complaint is signed by the Attorney holder on behalf of the payee­complainant, there is no reason why the Attorney holder cannot be examined as the complainant." 28. In view of above discussed case law, this court is of the <span class="hidden_text" id="span_24">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 25 of 30</span> opinion that if POA holder has personal knowledge of the case he can very well depose on behalf of the complainant. Both the complaints have been filed in the name of the complainant. In fact CC No. 1303/1 was filed by the complainant and subsequently he authorized his brother to pursue the present complaint. The GPA which is on record Ex. CW 1/1 has been duly proved vide which the Attorney has been authorized by the complainant to proceed with the present complaint and also to depose on his behalf. It has been very categorically stated in the affidavit filed by the Attorney holder that he has personal knowledge of the facts of the case and the onus was on the accused to show that the Attorney holder lacked personal knowledge so as to negate the evidence led by the attorney holder. In fact this issue has already been decided by this court vide order dated 31.03.2011. In that order Ld. Predecessor had allowed the Attorney holder to depose as complainant witness relying on the very judgment referred to by Ld. Counsel for the accused. Therefore, after perusing both the judgments this court is of the opinion that both the complaints fall within the ambit of the observations made by Hon'ble Supreme Court and as the Attorney holder had the personal knowledge of the facts of the case, this contention of the accused is <span class="hidden_text" id="span_25">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 26 of 30</span> also overruled. Even though some inconsistencies have come in the deposition of the complainant but it can not be said to be fatal to the case of the complainant. The Attorney holder more or less has supported the case of the complainant. 29. I have gone through the case law referred to by Ld. Counsel for the accused. However, no case law referred to by Ld. Counsel for the accused comes to the aid of the accused as facts of those case law are apparently distinguishable from the facts under consideration. 30. The decision in the case G. Panakhakshi Amma Vs. Mathai Mathew (supra) referred to by the accused is not applicable in the present case as the same is related to chit fund transactions between the parties and the complainant was a money lender who failed to produce his books of accounts. 31. In Sanjay Mishra Vs. Ms. Kanishka Kapoor @ Nikki & Anr (supra) the case was of advancing a friendly loan of Rs.15 lakhs to the accused persons and it was agreed between the parties that the accused shall repay the amount within 3 months. In this case also the <span class="hidden_text" id="span_26">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 27 of 30</span> complainant had not mentioned the transaction of loan in his ITR and therefore the complaint of the complainant was dismissed. 32. However, both the above case law are distinguishable from the facts in hand as the present complaint is regarding partnership deed executed between the parties and the same has been admitted by the accused. There is no dispute as to existence of partnership between the parties. Moreover the accused has himself admitted that some amount was paid by the complainant for the purpose of partnership business. The present case is neither a case of friendly loan nor of chit fund transaction. Moreover the complainant has produced copy of statement of accounts of the relevant period which corroborated the case of the complainant to some extent. In fact the accused has himself admitted the case of the complainant to some extent and defence placed by him seems to be just an afterthought. Further the accused has failed to satisfactorily explain as to how these cheques came into the possession of the complainant. 33. The other case law P. Venugopal Vs. Madan P Sarthi (supra), M.S. Narayana Menon Vs. State of Kerala (supra) and Bharat Barrel & Drum Manufacturing Company Vs. Amin Chand <span class="hidden_text" id="span_27">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 28 of 30</span> Pyare Lal (supra) are also of no assistance to the accused. In fact observations made in those case law go against the accused. In Bharat Barrel's case it was observed by Hon'ble Supreme Court as under: "The burden upon the defendant of proving the non­existence of the consideration can be either direct or by brining on record the preponderance of probabilities by reference to the circumstances upon which he relies. In such an event, the plaintiff is entitled under law to rely upon all the evidence led in the case including that of the plaintiff as well. In case, where the defendant fails to discharge the initial onus of proof by showing the non­existence of the consideration, the plaintiff would invariably be held entitled to the benefit of presumption arising under Section 118(a) in his favour. The court may not insist upon the defendant to disprove the existence of consideration by leading direct evidence as the existence of negative evidence is neither possible nor contemplated and even if led, is to be seen with a doubt. The bare denial of the passing of the consideration apparently does not appear to be any defence. Something which is probable has to be brought on record for getting the benefit of shifting the onus of proving to the plaintiff. To disprove the presumption, the defendant has to bring on record such facts and circumstances upon consideration of which the court may either believe that the consideration did not exist or its non­ existence was so probable that a prudent man would, under the circumstances of the case, shall act upon the plea that it did not exist.". FINAL ORDER. 34. In view of above it can be said that the story put forth <span class="hidden_text" id="span_28">Case No. 1149/1 &amp; 1303/1 Page 29 of 30</span> by the complainant is more believable. Whereas the story put forth by the accused does not inspire confidence and the accused has failed to rebut the presumptions raised against him under Section 118 & 139 of Negotiable Instruments Act. Accordingly, the accused is convicted under Section 138 of Negotiable Instruments Act.
