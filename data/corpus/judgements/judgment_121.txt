All the four cases are being disposed off vide a common judgment as the facts are the same and even the interconnected, therefore, all the cases are disposed off to avoid the repetition and also for the sake of brevity. Vide order dated 03.04.2007 all the four cases were clubbed together and CC no.207/05/03 was treated as the main case Therefore, by way of the present judgment, I shall dispose off four complaint cases filed under section 138 Negotiable Instrument Act, 1981 (as amended upto date). It is pertinent to mention here that each of the aforesaid four cases, is having two cheques, the details of which are given (casewise) as under : CC No. 207/1 Ch no. 500047 dt. 04.04.2003 Rs.2lacs Ch no. 500077 dt. 10.04.2003 Rs.1.5lac CC No. 206/1 Ch no. 500054 dt. 29.05.2003 Rs.1lac Ch no. 500053 dt. 20.05.2003 Rs.1lac CC No. 205/1 Ch no. 500048 dt. 17.04.2003 Rs.1lac Ch no. 500050 dt. 22.04.2003 Rs.2lacs CC No. 204/1 Ch no. 500052 dt. 15.05.2003 Rs.1lac Ch no. 500051 dt. 06.05.2003 Rs.1lac CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... 2. That the facts in brief necessary for the disposal of the present case, as per the averments in the complaint, are that the accused no.1 is a partnership firm and the accused no.2 to 7 are the partners of the same. That on representation made by the accused persons, the complainant gave a loan of Rs.10.5lacs and the accused persons promised and agreed to return the same alongwith interest @ 15.6% pa. That towards the discharge of the liability, the accused persons issued eight cheques. 3. That all the cheques on presentation to the concerned bank got dishonoured due to insufficiency of funds. That the complainant immediately contacted the accused persons and informed them about the dishonour of the cheques and also asked for the payment of the cheque amount. That thereafter legal notice dated 05.09.2003 under registered AD cover as well as UPC was served upon the accused persons but the accused persons did not make any payment. That as a result of which the complainant has filed the instant complaint for prosecution of the accused persons under section 138 Negotiable Instrument Act. 4. After hearing the Ld. counsel for the complainant and considering the entire material and documents on record, summons were issued against the accused persons vide order dated 21.02.2004. On appearance of the accused persons, a separate notice under section 251 CrPC dated 27.09.2006 was served upon the accused persons to which they pleaded not guilty and claimed trial. 5. In order to prove his case, the complainant has got CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... examined three witnesses including himself as CW3, Sh. Jagdish Raj Sahrma as CW1 and Sh. Kaushik Sur as CW2 in support of the contents of his complaint. 6. In his explanation u/s 313 CrPC, it was stated by accused Mayur Pasricha that he had not issued the cheques in question to the complainant. It was further submitted that he had not received any legal notice. It was further submitted that the cheques in question were misused by the complainant or that he never took any loan from the complainant and also did not owe any liability towards him. The accused persons opted not to lead DE in support of their contentions. Facts in issues 7. In order to have the positive outcome in his favour, the complainant was required to show that the cheques Ex.CW1/1 to Ex.CW1/2 (in each case) were given by the accused in discharge of their liabilities which were dishonoured vide returning memos whereafter the accused had also failed to comply with the requirements of the legal notices Ex. CW1/5 (in each case). 8. On the other hand, the accused persons were required to show their defence on the scale of preponderance of the probabilities that they are not liable to the amount involved in the complaints. Legal Prepositions 9. The presumptions provided U/s 118 NI Act and 139 NI Act would come to the rescue of the complainant once the execution of the cheque in question is proved on record. CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... 10. As per section 118 NI Act, it is to be presumed in favour of the complainant during the trial that the cheque in question was given against consideration by the accused and that the complainant was the holder of the said cheque in due course. Further as per Sec. 139 NI Act, it is to be presumed in favour of the complainant during the trial that the cheque in question was received by the complainant against a legally enforceable debt or liability (Refer :- "Rangappa Vs. Sri Mohan" SLP (Crl.) 407/06, Dated:- 07.05.2010). 11. It is well settled that both the aforesaid presumptions U/s 118 & 139 NI Act are rebuttable in nature and the onus to rebut the same squarely rests upon the accused. 12. The accused can rebut these presumptions not merely by examining his own witnesses but also through the cross examination of the complainant and his witnesses thereby bringing on record through the entire evidence available on record (inclusive of complainant's evidence and defence evidence, if any), that the complainant was a liar, that their was no existing liabilities between the parties and that the cheque in question was misused. It must be kept in mind that once evidence is brought on record from both sides, it becomes an evidence of the case and court can draw inferences from the said entire evidence either in favour or against any of the parties. Evidence is a complainant's evidence and accused's evidence only for the purposes of identifying it, but once it is adduced in the case, it becomes the evidence of the case and then the same has to be read as a whole. The court can not read the evidence of the complainant only to the extent it CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... favours the complainant and overlook the remaining evidence which supports the accused merely on the ground that it is the complainant's evidence. Similarly, from the evidence adduced by the accused, the court can draw inferences either in favour of the complainant or against the accused. The accused has a right to argue his case even on the basis of the cross examination of the complainant & his witnesses to show to the court that there existed no legally recoverable debt or liability between the parties. In order to rebut the legal presumption in question, it emerges that the accused need not require direct evidence to disprove the existence of consideration. 13. Preponderance of probabilities is the standard of proof upon the accused to rebut the above presumptions, which is not as high as that of the prosecution whereby the accused is only required to show the existence of a probable defence so as to rebut the above presumptions. If the accused succeeds in raising a probable defence by referring to his own evidence (if any) and from the evidence of the complainant, then the onus would shift on to the complainant, who then would have to show beyond reasonable doubt the existence of consideration/existence of a legally recoverable debt or liability in respect of the cheque in question. Appreciation of Evidence 14. Complainant lays foundation of his case on the ground that accused no.1 is a partnership firm and accused no.2 to 7 are the partners of the same. That on representation made by the accused persons, he had given a sum of Rs.10.5lacs to the accused persons and CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... the same was agreed to be returned alongwith interest @ 15.6% p.a. and in discharge of their liability, the cheques in question were issued by the accused persons. The cheques issued by the accused persons got dishonoured due to insufficiency of funds. Thereafter, inspite of receiving legal notice accused failed to pay the amount involved in the present complaint. 15. Sh. Vinod Mahajan (CW3) in his examination-in-chief reiterated the contents of the complaint on oath before the court and filed an affidavit in evidence. While under cross-examination, it was admitted by the complainant (CW3) that as per tax returns his income during those period was not been mentioned as Rs.10/12lacs per year. It was deposed by the witness that he was leading a retired life. That he was not having any independent source of income except few tuitions. That his income from the tuitions was hardly Rs.1,000/- to Rs.1200/- per month. It was further deposed by the witness that he do not remember whether as per ITR his income exceeded Rs.2lacs or not. That the entire transaction took place in the mid of January, 2003. That he do not remember how much money was lying in his bank account during the said period. It was further deposed by the witness that he has not shown the transaction pertaining to the present case in his ITR till date. That in the year 2003 his source of income was from tuitions and also the help from his parents. That he had not disclosed his source of income from the tuitions to any statutory authority including the IT Department. That he do not remember, if any amount was withdrawn from his bank. That he had no transaction with Gayatri Enterprises except the one in CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... question. 16. Sh. Jagdish Raj Sharma (CW1) is the official from the bank to prove the statement of account of the complainant. Sh. Kaushik Sur (CW2) is the bank official, who proved the bank memos. 17. The defence set up by the accused persons in the present case is that they have neither borrowed any loan from the complainant nor issued the cheque in question to the complainant. Further defence of the accused is that the statutory legal notice was not served upon them and that the cheques in question, lying in the custody of the complainant for the purpose of negotiation, were misused by him after filling up the same in his hand. 18. It is argued by the Ld. counsel for complainant that from evidence on record, the complainant has proved that cheque in question were signed by the accused which were dishonoured vide memo and despite the legal notice accused did not make the payment. It is argued that during the cross examination of complainant nothing material has come out and the complainant has been able to prove his case. It is further argued that accused has failed to discharge the burden upon him to rebutt the presumption in favour of the complainant under the Act. 19. Now the question is whether the complainant proved his case, that whether the amount was legally enforceable debt. The offence under Section 138 of the Act is a technical offence and the complainant is only supposed to prove that the cheque issued by the accused was dishonoured, his statement that cheque was issued against liability or CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... debt is sufficient proof of the debt or liability and the onus shifts to the accused to show the circumstances under which the cheque was issued and this could be proved by the accused only by way of cogent evidence. 20. The accused is not expected to prove his defence beyond reasonable doubt as is expected of the complainant in a criminal trial. The accused may adduce direct evidence/evidence on preponderance of probabilities to prove that cheque in question was not supported by consideration and that there was no debt or liability to be discharged by him. However, there is no need that the accused should disprove the non existence of consideration and debt by leading direct evidence because the existence of negative evidence is neither possible nor contemplated. At the same time, it is clear that bare denial of the passing of the consideration and existence of debt, apparently would not serve the purpose of the accused. Something which is probable has to be brought on record for getting the burden of proof shifted to the complainant. 21. As per complainant, on representation he had given a loan of Rs.10.5lacs to the accused persons and they had promised to repay the same alongwith interest @ 15.6% p.a. in discharge of their liability. The accused did not admit the issuance of the cheques in question to the complainant. It is the defence taken by the accused that the cheques in question were lying in the custody of the complainant for the purpose of negotiation and the complainant mischievously misused the same after filling up the contents of the same and that he did not owe any liability towards the complainant. CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... Moreover, the loan amount allegedly given by the complainant to the accused was a large amount and was not payable within few months. The failure on the part of the complainant to disclose the same in his income tax returns or the books of accounts is sufficient to rebut the presumption u/s 139 of NI Act. However, he failed to furnish on record the income tax returns for the relevant year. 22. It is well settled law that in a criminal case, prosecution has to stand upon its own legs and it cannot take the advantage of the fact that accused has not led cogent defence evidence or accused is having a weak defence. The defence raised by the accused along with the evidence placed on record are cogent proof in support of the defence version makes it highly probable that the cheques in question were not issued towards the discharge of friendly loan liability as alleged by the complainant and at the same time it weakens the foundation fabrics of the present complaint case. The suspicious circumstances rising in the present case will certainly give advantage to the accused. Moreover, the complainant has not come to the court with clean hands and his conduct is not of a prudent man. Hence, in the present case, the accused raises a cogent suspicious circumstances in the version of the complainant which belies the foundation of the present complaint case and same is sufficient to rebut the presumption that the cheques in question were not for discharging the legal liability. Conclusion 23. I have gone through the entire material on record carefully and heard the arguments advance by the counsel for parties at length. After analyzing the evidence led by the parties, I am of the opinion that CC No. 207/1, 206/1, 205/1 & 204/1 Contd..... accused has been successful in rebutting the presumptions under NI Act by raising cogent suspicion in the version of complainant and on the scale of preponderance of probabilities that the cheques in question may not be given to the complainant as alleged by him. 24. The rebuttal does not have to be conclusively established but such evidence must be adduced before the court in support of the defence that Court must either believe the defence to exist or consider its existence to be reasonably probable. In the present case accused has not only been able to raise a probable defence and adduced sufficient evidence to prove his defence but also been able to succeed in shaking the very foundation of the fabrics of the case of the complainant. 25. Once the accused has discharged his initial burden of proving its defence the onus rebutted back on the complainant. However in the present case, complainant has miserably failed to prove the case beyond reasonable doubt. 26. Therefore, from the above discussions, this court is of considered view that in the circumstances appearing from the record of the case, the accused is entitled to the benefit of doubt. Accordingly, the accused is entitled to be acquitted and hereby stands acquitted of the offence under Section 138 of Negotiable Instrument Act. A copy of the judgment be placed in each case file.
