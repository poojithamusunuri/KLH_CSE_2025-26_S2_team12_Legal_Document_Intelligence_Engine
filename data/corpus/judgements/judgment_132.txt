APPRECIATION OF EVIDENCE IN THE LIGHT OF INGREDIENTS OF SECTION 138 NI ACT AND THEB DEFENCE RAISED BY THE ACCUSED 8. It is not disputed by the accused that the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 are drawn on the account maintained by him. Further signatures on the cheques in question has also not been disputed by the accused. The presentation, dishonor of the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 and the cheque returning memo/letter Ex. CW1/6 has not been challenged by the accused. Therefore, considering the entire evidence on record it stands duly proved that the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 were dishonored vide cheque returning memo Ex. CW1/6 all with remarks "Funds Insufficient". Further throughout the trial, the receipt of the legal demand notice Ex. CW1/7 has also been admitted by the accused. Therefore, it also stands proved that the legal demand notice Ex. CW1/7 was duly served upon the accused. 9. In the instant case only one defence has been taken by the accused i.e. he has not given the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 to the complainant in discharge of his loan liability and the same were given by him to the complainant in blank signed manner as security at the time of taking of the loan. 10. Now coming to the first limb of the defence taken by the accused that apart from the signatures no other particular in the cheque's in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 has been filled by him. It is not disputed by the accused that the Cheques in Question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 are drawn on the account maintained by him. Further signature on the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 has also not been disputed by the accused. Throughout the trial the accused has specifically contended that except signatures other particulars in the CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 cheque's in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 has not been filled by him. During the course of arguments Ld. Counsel for the accused also contended that the except signatures other particulars on the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 has not been filled by the accused. It is trite to say that by putting the name and date there is no material alteration on the cheque U/s 87 of the NI Act. There is no rule of banking business that the name of the payee as well as the amount should be written by the drawer himself as no law provides that in case of cheque the entire body has to be written by the drawer only. Therefore, once the signature on the cheque in question is admitted, as has happened in the instant case the plea that particulars in the cheques in question has not been filled by the drawer is of no consequence. Therefore the arguments and contentions of the accused in this regard hold no ground. 11. Now coming to the second limb of the defence taken by the accused i.e. He has not given the cheques in question Ex. CW1/3, Ex. CW1/4 and Ex. CW1/5 to the Complainant in discharge of his loan liability and the same were given by him to the complainant as security at the time of taking of the loan. No doubt the crux of penal liability under Section 138 of the NI Act is that the Cheque in question must be issued for the discharge of any legal debt or liability. Existing of legal debt or liability is sine qua non for constituting the offence. 12. Before I advert to the above said line of defence taken by the accused, it is imperative for me to notice the presumptions under Sections 118(a) and 139 of the NI Act which read as under: "118. Presumptions as to negotiable instruments.--Until the contrary is proved, the following presumptions shall be made­ (a) of consideration.--that every negotiable instrument was made or drawn for consideration, and that every such instrument when it has been accepted, indorsed, negotiat­ ed or transferred, was accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred for consideration;" "139. Presumption in favour of holder.--It shall be pre­ sumed, unless the contrary is proved, that the holder of a cheque received the cheque, of the nature referred to in Section 138 for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any debt or other liability. CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 13. The Division bench of Hon'ble Supreme Court in KRISHNA JANARDHAN BHAT Vs. DATTATRAYA G. HEGDE, (2008) 4 SCC 54, has held that the accused can discharge the burden of presumption U/s 118 and U/s 139 of the NI Act by raising a probabale defence on the strength of preponderance of probabilites. 14. In RANGAPPA Vs. SRI MOHAN AIR 2010 SC 1898 the Hon'ble Supreme Court has held that it is a settled position that when an accused has to rebut the presumption under Section 139, the standard of proof for doing so is that of "preponderance of probabilities". The accused can rely on the materials submitted by the complainant in order to raise such a de­ fence. 15. From the reading of the above said provisions and the judg­ ments the position of law which emerges is that once execution of the promissory note is admitted, the presumption under Section 118(a) and Section 139 of the NI Act would arise that it is supported by a consideration. Such presumptions are rebuttable. The accused can prove the non­exis­ tence of a consideration by raising a probable defence. If the accused dis­ charges the initial onus of proof by showing that the existence of considera­ tion was improbable or doubtful or the same was illegal, the onus would shift to the Complainant who will be obliged to prove it as a matter of fact and upon its failure to prove would disentitle him to the grant of relief on the basis of the negotiable instrument. The burden upon the accused of proving the non­existence of the consideration can be either direct or by bringing on record the preponderance of probabilities by reference to the circumstances upon which he relies. In case, where the accused fails to dis­ charge the initial onus of proof by showing the non­existence of the consid­ eration, the complainant would invariably be held entitled to the benefit of presumption arising under Section 118(a) and Section 139 of the NI Act in his favour. 16. Now coming to the instant case, in the case in hand, it is the CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 case to the complainant that the accused has availed housing loan facility from the complainant and in discharge of his part liability he has issued the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5. The accused has refuted the stand of the complainant and has examined himself as DW­1 and admitted that he has taken loan from the complainant but with respect to the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5, it is stated by the accused that the same were given by him as a security to the complainant at the time of taking of the loan. It is also stated by the accused that he has applied for the home loan with the complainant bank for the purchase of the Flat no. 4051 in Golf Course Sehkari Awas Samiti Ltd, Noida. It is further submitted by the accused that he was granted loan by the complainant vide loan account no. PH260100170449 and has also signed loan agreement and the tripartite agreement Ex.CW1/12. It is further submitted by the accused that after completion of documents, the complainant has directly issued the disbursal cheque Ex. AW1/A to the society / builder i.e. The Golf Course Sehkahri Awas Samiti Ltd. It is also stated by the accused that thereafter he came to know that the project of the builder is fake and accordingly he surrendered the said unit to the builder. The accused also stated that he has intimated the said fact to the complainant bank vide letter Ex. AW1/C (colly) dated 28.01.2012 and requested the complainant for stop presenting his ECS/ Cheque. The accused further stated that the complainant has misused his security cheque and has filed the present complaint case. 17. Interestingly, perusal of the document Ex. AW1/B placed on record by DW­1 (accused) reveals that the same is acceptance of the surrender of the flat by the builder and the loan account no. PH1260100170449 is also mentioned in the said letter. Further, the said letter is dated 01.03.2011, therefore, it is evident that the flat unit for purchase of which the loan was taken by the accused was surrendered by the accused to the builder and the said surrender was also accepted by the builder on 01.03.2011. Further clause 4 of the tripartite agreement Ex.CW1/12 reveals that in case the borrower fails to honor the commitment towards the builder the developer / builder shall inform the bank and the bank shall have the right to pay the sale consideration and get the same registered either in CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 banks name or in the name of its nominee and in the event of borrower defaulting in making the payment of installment to the bank than in such situation the bank shall have right to inform the builder and accordingly the bank have the right to get the agreement executed between the builder and the borrower cancelled and further the bank would have the right to pay the sale consideration and get the property registered either in the banks name or in the name of its nominee. Admittedly, the tripartite agreement Ex.CW1/12 is dated 24.08.2009 and the accused has surrendered his flat to the builder which is acknowledged by the builder vide its letter dated 01.03.2011. Therefore, it is evident that the tripartite agreement was breached by the accused and it was for the complainant bank to pursue its remedy in terms of the agreement. Now what needs to be considered that whether the complainant bank was aware about the surrender of the financed unit (Flat) by the accused to the builder. It interesting to see that the accused has tendered in evidence a letter dated 28.01.2012 along with the postal receipts and postal tracking report Ex. AW1/C (Colly.) written by the accused to the recovery head of the complainant bank intimating him about surrender and further requesting the complainant not to present his ECS / cheques. The postal tracking report placed by the accused shows the status of the said letter as delivered to the complainant. Thereby making it clear that the complainant bank was very much aware about the surrender of the unit which was got financed by the accused. Further, the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 dated 01.11.2011, 01.12.2011 & 01.01.2012 were dishonored vide cheque returning memo Ex. CW1/6 dated 19.03.2012 whereas the letter dated 28.01.2012 Ex. AW1/C (Colly.) which is was written by the accused to the recovery head of the complainant bank was much prior to the dishonour of the cheques in question. Thereby making it evident clear that the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 were in possession of the complainant bank and were presented by the complainant bank despite having knowledge about surrender of the unit which was got financed by the accused otherwise there was occasion for the accused to request the complainant not to present his cheques. 18. Further more, it is also highly inconceivable that when the CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 accused has surrendered the flat to the builder which has been acknowledged by the builder vide its letter Ex. AW1/B dated on 01.03.2011, then the accused would issue the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 are dated 01.11.2011, 01.12.2011 & 01.01.2012. to the complainant i.e. Even after surrendering the said flat against which he has taken loan from the complainant. Further the accused has consistently stated that he used to make payment of his EMI's through ECS. The complainant has neither placed the statement of account of the accused on record to show as to how the complainant was receiving the EMI's from the accused. Further, even the home loan agreement Ex. CW1/11 placed on record by the complainant also is silent as to the mode of the payment. Therefore, from the evidence on record the submissions of the accused that the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 were given by him as a security at the time of taking of the loan and the complainant has misused the same finds more force and seems more probable. 19. Further, even believing that the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 dated 01.11.2011, 01.12.2011 & 01.01.2012 were issued as post­dated cheques pertaining to the regular EMI's, still it is pertinent to note that the said unit for which the loan was taken by the accused has been surrendered by the accused which is duly acknowledged by the letter of the builder Ex. AW1/B dated 01.03.2011. It is trite to say that the agreement on the basis of which the cheques has been issued, has been breached by the complainant and the flat unit has also been surrendered by the complainant to the builder much prior to the presentation of the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 dated 01.11.2011, 01.12.2011 & 01.01.2012 and the cheques which have been subsequently put for clearance no doubt would not fall within the ambit of cheques issued in discharged of his liability as the same becomes instrument without consideration and the remedy available to the complainant bank would be to realize the payment in terms of the agreement. 20. In a case of this nature, where the chances of false implication cannot be ruled out, the background fact and the conduct of the parties together CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 with their legal requirements are required to be taken into consideration. All the aforesaid circumstances/factors rebut the presumptions provided under Section 139 of the NI Act because they raise credible doubt over the veracity of the complainant's claims and satisfy the test of rebuttal on the strength of preponderance of probabilities thereby weakening the claim of the Complainant. Furthermore a statutory presumption under Section 139 of the NI Act though has an evidentiary value, the question as to whether the presumption stood rebutted or not must be determined keeping in view the other evidences on record. The defence raised by the accused is sufficient to discharge the initial burden of proof raised on him. This court do not find any force in the arguments advanced by the learned counsel for the complainant that the accused has failed to rebut the presumption under Section 139 of the NI Act. Presumptions are not in itself evidence but it only enables a party in whose favour it exists to show that he has a prima facie case. In the case in hand the presumptions under section 139 of the NI Act alone cannot be the sole basis to prove the case of the complainant. The materials and evidence available on the record brought by the complainant do not bridge the gap. Therefore, I hereby hold that the complainant has not been able to prove and substantiate the allegations that the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 were issued by the accused in discharge of his legal liability. 21. Furthermore, mere non payment of cheques amount within 15 days of receipt of legal notice is not itself sufficient to fasten any liability U/s 138 of the NI Act as it is established that the cheques in question Ex.CW1/3, Ex.CW1/4 & Ex.CW1/5 were not issued by the accused in discharge of his legal liability. 22. Accordingly, In view of the facts, evidence lead by both the parties coupled with my discussion above, I hereby hold that the Complainant has not been able to prove and substantiate its allegations against the accused (Sunil Dutt Shukla) and all the ingredients for the offence under section 138 of the NI Act have not been proved against the accused. Accordingly, accused Sunil Dutt Shukla S/o Sh. Diwakar Dutt Shukla is hereby acquitted CC No. 2264/12 Axis Bank Ltd. Vs Sunil Dutt Shukla 12/12 for the offence u/s 138 of the NI Act.
