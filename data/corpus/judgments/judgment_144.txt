IN THE COURT OF METROPOLITAN MAGISTRATE MUNICIPAL MAGISTRATE­01, KKD, EAST, NEW DELHI Presided by : Ms. Deepti Devesh Complaint No. 970/13 Unique ID No. 02402R0397602013 PS: Preet Vihar Kalim Javed, S/o Sh. Naseer Ahmad, R/o H.No. 44, J - Extn. Gali No.8, Laxmi Nagar Delhi­110092 ........Complainant VERSUS Smt. Daljeet Kaur, W/o Sh. Baljinder Singh, R/o H.No.2, Sukh Vihar, Delhi­51 ........Accused Complaint under section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Ac t Offence complained of : U/s 138 NI Act Date of commission of offence : 12.11.2013 Plea of Accused : Not guilty Final Arguments heard & Concluded on : 30.09.2014 Date of decision of the case : 21.10.2014 Final Order : Conviction BRIEF FACTS AND REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE: 1 Vide this Judgment I shall finally dispose off this complaint U/s 138 NI Act by complainant Sh. Kalim Javed against the accused Daljeet Kaur. The CC No. 970/13 page no. 1 /11 complainant's story in brief is that the accused approached the complainant for a friendly loan for a period of six months. On 05.12.2012 the complainant had given a loan of Rs.2,50,000/­ in cash to the accused with the stipulation of the accused that she would return the same within six months along with additional amount of Rs.50,000/­ to the complainant. The accused has also executed a written note in this respect. In order to repay the loan, the accused issued three post dated cheques bearing no. 545947 dated 23.09.2013 for Rs.1,00,000/­, bearing no.545948 dated 03.10.2013 of Rs.1,50,000/­ & bearing no. 545949 dated 12.10.2013 of Rs.50,000/­, all are drawn on SBI, Swasthya Vihar branch in favor of the complainant and had assured the complainant that the same shall be honored on their presentation. The said cheques were presented by the complainant which were returned vide return memo dated 11.10.2013, 11.10.2013 & 18.10.2013 respectively with endorsement of "funds insufficient". Thereafter the complainant issued legal notice dated 26.10.2013. The complainant has also stated that despite service of the legal notice the accused did not make any payment in discharge of her liability. 2 After the filing of the complaint and closing of pre­summoning evidence, vide order dated 10.12.2013, summons were issued to the accused for the offence U/s 138 NI Act. The accused was admitted to bail on 25.03.2014 which was confirmed on 28.04.2014. Thereafter notice U/s 251 Cr. PC was framed upon the accused on 25.03.2014 to which she did not plead guilty and claimed trial. 3 Application U/s 145(2) NI Act was filed by the accused on 16.05.2014, which was allowed on the same day. Thereafter the matter was fixed for CE. 4 In order to prove its case, the complainant examined himself as CW­1 who reiterated the contents of the complaint on Oath and filed evidence by way of affidavit. CW­1 exhibited handwritten note executed by the accused in favour of the complainant as Ex. CW1/1, original cheques bearing no. 545947 dated CC No. 970/13 page no. 2 /11 23.09.2013 as Ex. CW1/2, no.545948 as Ex. CW1/3 & no. 545949 as Ex. CW1/4, return memos dated 11.10.2013 as Ex. CW1/5 & Ex. CW1/6, dated 18.10.2013 as Ex. CW1/7, legal notice dated 26.10.2013 as Ex. CW1/8, Registered post receipts as Ex. CW1/9 (colly) & Net tracking report as Ex. CW1/10. Thereafter CW­1 was cross examined and discharged. 5 After that on 22.07.2014 the accused was examined U/s 313 Cr. PC and her statement was duly recorded. All the incriminating evidence were put to the accused, to which the accused denied taking any loan from the complainant. The accused has further stated that she had given the cheques in question as security to the complainant for a committee to which she was a party and she has no liability towards the complainant. It is further submitted by her that the complainant has also gotten her signature on a blank paper which has also been misused by the complainant. She also denied receiving any legal notice from the complainant and further denied her liability towards the complainant. The accused also stated that she does not want to lead defence evidence and matter was fixed for final arguments. However, further clarification was sought from the accused and her further statement U/s 311 Cr. Read with section 313 Cr. PC was recorded on 30.09.2014, in which the accused stated that Ex. CW1/1 was written by her in her handwriting and she also admitted her signature on Ex. CW1/1. She further stated that she had given Ex. CW1/1 to the complainant after he pressurize her by threatening to tell her husband about the blank signed security cheques and the committee. 6 Final arguments were addressed and heard on behalf of both the parties. Ld. Counsel for the complainant has reiterated the averments made in the complaint. He has argued that the accused has failed to disclose any defence. Therefore all ingredients of U/s 138 NI Act stands fulfilled and the accused is liable to be convicted. 7 On the other hand, the Counsel for the accused has argued that the CC No. 970/13 page no. 3 /11 present complaint should be dismissed as there is no legal liability upon the accused to pay the complainant as no loan of Rs.2,50,000/­ was ever taken by the accused from the complainant and that the cheques in question are the security cheques given to the complainant. Counsel for the accused has relied upon following judgments: a) Kumar Exports v. Sharma Carpets Crl 2045/2008, b) Vipin Kumar Gupta v. Vipin Gupta Crl. L.P. 461/2011, c) Ravi Kuamr D. v. State of Delhi & Anr in Crl M.C. NO. 4378/2009 decided on 01.03.2011. 8 Arguments advanced by both the parties have been heard at length. I have also perused the judicial record. 9 For offence U/s 138 NI Act the necessary ingredients to be proved are: a) That the accused had a legal liability / debt towards the complainant. b) That the accused issued a cheque in discharge of the said legal liability / debt. c) That the accused was maintaining a bank account for the cheque issued by him. d) That the cheque was presented by the complainant within its validity period. e) That the cheque was dishonored on his presentation. f) That the complainant issued legal demand notice within 30 days from the date of dishonor of the cheque. g) That the accused failed to pay within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice. If the above ingredients are proved then an offence punishable U/s 138 NI Act is committed. 10 In the present case, the accused has stated in the notice framed U/s CC No. 970/13 page no. 4 /11 251 Cr. PC that she had signed the cheques in question but had given the same to the complainant in blank signed condition as security on his demand for a loan of Rs.1,50,000/­ taken from him about an year and half ago. The accused admitted her liability to pay Rs.1,50,000/­ to the complainant. The accused also stated that she did not receive legal notice from the complainant but that must have been received by her husband and her child. However, the accused has contradicted herself in her statement U/s 313 Cr. PC wherein she has firstly, stated that the cheques in question were given as security to the complainant for a committee of which she was a member and there is no mention of any loan of Rs.1,50,000/­ in the same, secondly she has outrightly denied receiving the legal notice from the complainant. However, the statutory presumption of delivery of legal notice is in favour of the complainant and also as per the Judgment of Hon'ble supreme court in C.C. Alavi Haji v. P. Mohammad (2007) 6 SCC 555, it is not the case of the accused that she was willing to make payment of the cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of the summons and therefore, it is immaterial whether the legal notice was received or not by the accused. Thus the only disputed question to be decided whether the cheques in question were issued in discharge of legal liability / debt to the complainant. 11 Now, the question of liability of the accused to pay the complainant shall be dealt with. In the present case the complainant has averred that the cheques in question were issued against discharge of liability arising out of loan transaction between the parties. Since the cheques in question are bearing the name of complainant and the present complaint has been filed by the complainant in his own name therefore, presumption arises U/s 118­A & 139 NI Act in favour of the complainant that the cheques were issued in discharge of a legal liability. Thus, the onus to rebut this presumption is on the accused. The accused has disputed her legal liability to make payment on the ground that the cheques in question were security cheques and that the same have been misused by the complainant. CC No. 970/13 page no. 5 /11 12 It is true that for offence U/s 138 NI Act, the accused is not expected to prove his defense beyond reasonable doubt in order to rebut the statutory presumption. Same has been held by Hon'ble Supreme Court in Kumar Exports v. Sharma Carpets (2009) 2 SCC 513. It is also well settled law that in order to rebut the statutory presumption in favour of the complainant, the accused need not examine himself in evidence or adduce any other defence evidence also. The accused can rebut the statutory presumption on the material already available on record. The same has been held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Krishna Janardan Bhat v. Dattatraya G. Hegde 2008 Cri.L.J 1172 (SC). Now it will have to be examined whether the accused has been able to rebut the statutory presumption U/s 118 & 139 of NI Act. 13 The accused has stated during admission­denial U/s 294 Cr. PC that the details on the cheques in question have not been filled by her. However, this is not very relevant in proving the innocence of the accused as the law does not require for offence U/s 138 NI Act that the entire cheque has to be in the handwriting of the accused. Admission of signatures is sufficient to prove that the cheque in question must have been issued in discharge of some legal liability / debt. In Jaipal Singh Rana v. Swaraj Pal 149 (2008) DLT 682, the Hon'ble Delhi High Court has held that there is no alteration in a cheque if the amount and the date are filled by somebody else apart from the signatory of the cheque. It was further held that there is no law that requires the filling up of entire cheque by drawer himself. Further, in Ravi Chopra v. State & Anr 2008 (2) LRC 118 (Del), it has been held that if a blank signed cheque is given then it is possible that the drawer has consented impliedly or expressly to filling up of the cheque by the payee on a later date. Further in Vijender Singh v. Eicher Motors Ltd & Anr decided by Hon'ble Delhi High Court on 05.05.2011, it was laid down that any person who issues blank signed cheque should understand the consequences of doing so. Therefore, in view of the above case laws, it is no defence to offence U/s 138 NI Act that the cheque in question was issued in blank CC No. 970/13 page no. 6 /11 signed condition. 14 Furthermore, the accused has not led any defence evidence to rebut the statutory presumption arising in favour of the complainant. It is also pertinent to note that the accused has contradicted herself in her notice U/s 251 Cr. PC and statement U/s 313 Cr. PC. At one place, she stated that she had taken a loan of Rs.1,50,000/­ from the complainant and the cheques in question were security for the said loan, while at another she stated that the cheques in question as security for a committee run by the complainant of which she was a member. In view of the contradiction, neither of the statement of the accused is reliable. Further, due to lack of any evidence adduced by the accused, neither the fact that the cheques in question were issued for a loan taken from the complainant nor the fact that the cheques in question were issued for a committee has been subjected to cross­examination and hence, can not be relied upon by the court. In absence of any evidence, bald statements made by the accused are not believable. Furthermore, the explanation given by the accused should not only be reasonable, but also true one. It has been held in V.S. Yadav v. Reena 2010 (4) JCC (NI) 323 that the statutory presumption in NI Act can not be rebutted on mere explanations given by the accused U/s 251 Cr. PC & 313 Cr. PC without leading any other cogent evidence. 15 Further, the accused has also contradicted herself as in her application U/s 145(2) NI Act she has stated that the complainant had obtained her signatures on blank paper while later on she has admitted her handwriting on Ex. CW1/1 as well as her signatures on Ex. CW1/1. Infact the accused has consistently stated that the complainant had obtained her signature on a blank signed paper and infact has also suggested to the complainant in his cross examination that Ex. CW1/1 was blank when the same was signed by the accused. However, in her statement U/s 311 Cr. PC read with U/s 313 Cr. PC she has admitted that Ex. CW1/1 is written in her own handwriting. Due to the contradictory stands of the accused, the accused has herself render herself CC No. 970/13 page no. 7 /11 unreliable and therefore, the story of the accused is not believable. 16 The accused has also not been able to demolish the complainant's story in his cross­examination. The complainant has mentioned the date of loan transaction in his complaint and has categorically stated that the accused has handedover the cheques in question which were undated on the same day of the loan transaction and also executed Ex. CW1/1 in favour of the complainant. He further, stated that after expiry of six months from the date of loan transaction, after repeated demand from the accused he filled up the dates on the cheques in question at the instance of the accused and then presented the same for encashment. The complainant has reiterated in his cross­examination that he had filled the cheques in question at the instance of the accused after the accused failed to make payment despite repeated demands by the complainant who visited house of the accused for demanding his money back in July, August & September 2013. He has further reiterated that since he knew the accused for the last 20 years and was having good relations with her, he has not made any police complaint against her. The complainant has not been cross examined on the aspect that he had filled the details on the cheques in question at the instance of the accused, while the complainant has stated the same in his evidence by way of evidence and also reiterated the same in his cross­ examination. As discussed earlier in Ravi Chopra v. State & Anr 2008 (2) LRC 118 (Del) offence U/s 138 NI Act arises even if the details on the cheque in question is filled by expressed or implied consent of the accused. The accused has only stated in her statement U/s 313 Cr. PC that the complainant presented cheques in question without informing her. Firstly, it is well settled that for offence U/s 138 NI Act no intimation has to be given to the accused / signatory of the cheque before presenting it for encashment. Secondly, statement made U/s 313 Cr. PC is not evidence and has also not been subjected to any cross­ examination. Thirdly, no evidence has been brought by the accused to show that the details on the cheques in question or the date on the cheques in question had not been filled at the instance of the accused and therefore, the accused has not CC No. 970/13 page no. 8 /11 been able to rebut the statutory presumption in favour of the complainant. 17 The complainant has admitted in his cross­examination that he is not an income tax payee and that he earns Rs.30,000/­ to Rs.35,000/­ per month. He further stated that he had advanced the loan of Rs.2,50,000/­ to the accused from his savings which he had accumulated for getting his son admission in B. Tech. It is not far­fetched to believe that a person earning Rs.30,000/­ to Rs.35,000/­ per month would not have savings of Rs.2,50,000/­. Further, the complainant has also produced handwritten receipt Ex. CW1/1 wherein the accused has admitted the loan of Rs.2,50,000/­ and has also undertaken to return the same along with Rs.50,000/­ in six months for which she had issued the cheques in question. Nothing has come in the cross­examination of the complainant which discredit the Ex. CW1/1. Infact, in the statement U/s 311 read with U/s 313 Cr. PC, the accused had admitted Ex. CW1/1 and has further stated she had written the same in her own handwriting and it bears her signatures. Although she had further stated that she had executed Ex. CW1/1 under coercion, however, Section 91 & 92 Indian Evidence Act 1872 precludes any oral evidence in contradiction evidence to documentary evidence except in certain circumstances. The present case is squarely out of those circumstances and therefore, the statement of the accused can not be relied upon. Further, the accused has not led any evidence to prove that Ex. CW1/1 was executed under pressure or coercion from the complainant. 18 The complainant has admitted in his cross­examination that he had filled the details on the cheques Ex. CW1/3 & Ex. CW1/4, but at the instance of the accused. However, as discussed earlier, who has filled the cheques is immaterial since the signatures on the cheques in question are not disputed. Counsel for the accused has argued that the complainant has contradicted himself in his cross­examination and therefore, an unreliable witness. He has argued that at one place the complainant has stated that he has not received any money from the accused while at another place he has admitted receipt of CC No. 970/13 page no. 9 /11 Rs.8000/­ paid by cheque by the accused. Even if this is taken to be contradiction in the testimony of the complainant, yet it is well settled that minor contradictions are immaterial if the overall testimony of the complainant is reliable and trustworthy. 19 The complainant has also stated in his cross­examination that he knows th accused for last 20 years who is a housewife. Nothing has come on record to disprove the friendly relations between the complainant and the accused and therefore a loan of Rs.2,50,000/­ for a period of six months to a person known for a period of last 20 years, at the time of her need, is not unbelievable. Further, the complainant is not an income tax payee and although the loan transaction may be in contravention of Section 269 SS Income Tax Act 1961, yet the same has not bearing on offence U/s 138 NI Act Any default in paying tax is a matter between the defaulter and revenue authorities and it is not a ground in law to deny avenues open to the complainant for recovering the loan amount. Same has been held in Krishna P. Murajkar v. Joe Ferrao 2013 ACD 942 (Bom). 20 Counsel for the accused has relied on several case laws in support of his contentions, however, the said case laws are distinguishable from the present complaint on the facts of the case and therefore, are inapplicable. 21 The Hon'ble High Court of Delhi has held in V.S. Yadav v. Reena 172 (2010) DLT 561 that the offence U/s 138 NI Act is a technical offence and the complainant is required only to prove that the cheque issued by the accused was dishonored, that the cheque was issued in discharge of legal liability / debt, that legal notice was sent by the complainant within 30 days of dishonor and that accused failed to pay the cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of legal notice. In view of the above discussion the complainant has discharged the burden upon CC No. 970/13 page no. 10 /11 him to prove the above said ingredients beyond reasonable doubt. Accordingly accused Accordingly, accused Smt. Daljeet Kaur, W/o Sh. Baljinder Singh, R/o H.No.2, Sukh Vihar, Delhi­51 stands convicted of the offence under section 138 NI Act. 22 Let a copy of the Judgment be supplied to the accused free of cost.
