IN THE COURT OF METROPOLITAN MAGISTRATE MUNICIPAL MAGISTRATE­01, KKD, EAST, NEW DELHI Presided by : Ms. Deepti Devesh Complaint No. 350/13 Unique ID No. 02402R0138602013 PS: Geeta Colony Radhey Shyam, S/o Sh. Khem Chand, R/o 10/31 Geeta Colony, Delhi­31 ........Complainant VERSUS Sh. Vicky Kapoor, S/o Sh. Roshan Lal Kapoor, R/o 131­B, Rani Garden, Delhi­31 ........Accused Complaint under section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Ac t Offence complained of : U/s 138 NI Act Date of commission of offence : 07.04.2013 Plea of Accused : Not guilty Final Arguments heard & Concluded on : 26.09.2014 Date of decision of the case : 29.10.2014 Final Order : Acquittal BRIEF FACTS AND REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE: 1 Vide this Judgment I shall finally dispose off this complaint U/s 138 NI Act by complainant Sh. Radhey Shyam against the accused Vicky Kapoor. The complainant's story in brief is that the accused approached the complainant, CC No. 350/13 page no. 1 /11 having friendly relations with the complainant and on 10.10.2012 had approached the complainant for a friendly loan of Rs.50,000/­ for a period of three months, which was advanced to the accused. In order to repay the loan, the accused issued a cheque bearing no. 361083 dated 07.03.2013 for Rs.50,000/­ drawn on Punjab & Sind Bank, Old Geeta Colony branch in favor of the complainant and had assured the complainant that the same shall be honored on its presentation. The said cheque was presented by the complainant which was returned vide return memo dated 08.03.2013 with endorsement of "funds insufficient". Thereafter, the complainant issued legal notice dated 20.03.2013. The complainant has also stated that despite service of the legal notice the accused did not make any payment in discharge of his liability. 2 After the filing of the complaint and closing of pre­summoning evidence, vide order dated 14.05.2013, summons were issued to the accused for the offence U/s 138 NI Act. The accused was admitted to bail on 05.06.2013. Thereafter notice U/s 251 Cr. PC was framed upon the accused on 05.06.2013 to which he did not plead guilty and claimed trial. 3 Application U/s 145(2) NI Act was filed by the accused on 05.07.2013, which was allowed on the same day as not opposed by the Counsel for the complainant. Thereafter, the matter was fixed for CE. 4 In order to prove its case, the complainant examined himself as CW­1 who reiterated the contents of the complaint on Oath and filed evidence by way of affidavit. CW­1 exhibited original cheque bearing no. 361083 dated 07.03.2013 as Ex. CW1/A, return memo dated 08.03.2013 as Ex. CW1/B and duplicate copy of return memo dated 08.03.2013 as Ex. CW1/B1, legal notice dated 21.03.2013 as Ex. CW1/C, Registered postal receipts as Ex. CW1/D & Ex. CW1/E and delivery report for legal notice as Mark­B. Thereafter, CW­1 was cross examined and discharged. CC No. 350/13 page no. 2 /11 5 After that on 16.01.2014 the accused was examined U/s 313 Cr. PC and his statement was duly recorded. All the incriminating evidence were put to the accused, to which the accused denied taking any loan from the complainant. The accused has further stated that he had given the cheque in question as security for a loan of Rs.30,000/­ taken by him from the complainant in April 2012. It is further stated that till August 2012 he had returned an amount of Rs.15,000/­ to the complainant and he also paid an interest of Rs.1000/­ per month to the complainant from April 2012 till August 2012. It is further stated by him that since he had paid half of the loan amount to the complainant, hence, requested the complainant to lower the interest amount but he did not agree to the same, hence, the complainant did not accept further payment from him. It is further stated by the accused that he is only liable to pay Rs.15,000/­ to the complainant and is still ready to pay the same to the complainant. He denied receiving legal notice from the complainant. The accused also stated that he wanted to lead defence evidence. 6 Accused examined himself as DW­1. He stated that in April 2012 he had taken a loan of Rs.30,000/­ from the complainant and at that time he had handed over a blank cheque to the complainant as security. In August 2012 he had returned Rs.15,000/­ to the complainant out of total loan of Rs.30,000/­ and he had also paid monthly interest of Rs. 1,000/­ to the complainant upto August 2012. Thereafter, he stopped making payment of the interest as he requested the complainant to reduce the interest amount from Rs. 1,000/­ to Rs.500/­ but the complainant did not agree to the same. He further stated that after receiving summons from the court on 30.5.2013, he called the complainant and recorded the telephonic conversation in which the complainant admitted that he had given only loan of Rs.30,000/­ and that he had filled the amount of Rs.50,000/­ in the cheque in question on the advice of his counsel. The accused also placed on record CD containing record of the conversation along with certificate U/s 65­B Indian Evidence Act and transcript of the conversation which is exhibited as Ex. DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages). Counsel for the complainant has objected to Ex. CC No. 350/13 page no. 3 /11 DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages) on the ground that the CD containing the telephonic conversation is not proved as per law. The said objection is decided in succeeding part of the present judgment. Thereafter, DW­1 was cross examined and discharged, defence evidence was closed and matter was fixed for final arguments. 7 Final arguments were addressed and heard on behalf of both the parties. Ld. Counsel for the complainant has reiterated the averments made in the complaint. He has argued that the accused has failed to disclose any defence. Therefore all ingredients of U/s 138 NI Act stands fulfilled and the accused is liable to be convicted. 8 On the other hand, the Counsel for the accused has argued that the present complaint should be dismissed as there is no legal liability upon the accused to pay the complainant as no loan of Rs.50,000/­ was ever taken by the accused from the complainant. Infact the accused handed over the cheque in question to the complainant as security for a loan of Rs.30,000/­. He has further argued that the complainant has infact demanded Rs.50,00,000/­ from the accused in his legal notice and therefore has contradicted himself and hence, the present complaint is liable to be dismissed. Counsel for the accused has further stated that the cheque in question has been misused by the complainant. 9 Arguments advanced by both the parties have been heard at length. I have also perused the judicial record. 10 For offence U/s 138 NI Act the necessary ingredients to be proved are: a) That the accused had a legal liability / debt towards the complainant. b) That the accused issued a cheque in discharge of the said legal liability / debt. c) That the accused was maintaining a bank account for the cheque CC No. 350/13 page no. 4 /11 issued by him. d) That the cheque was presented by the complainant within its validity period. e) That the cheque was dishonored on his presentation. f) That the complainant issued legal demand notice within 30 days from the date of dishonor of the cheque. g) That the accused failed to pay within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice. If the above ingredients are proved then an offence punishable U/s 138 NI Act is committed. 11 In the present case, the accused has stated in the notice framed U/s 251 Cr. PC that he has taken only a loan of Rs.30,000/­ from the complainant and has already repaid a sum of Rs.15,000/­ to the complainant. He stated further that he only signed on the cheque in question and other details have not been filled by him. He has reiterated the same in his statement U/s 313 Cr PC. He has also stated that the cheque in question was given as security to the complainant. It has been denied by the accused that he received legal notice from the complainant, although the statutory presumption of delivery of notice is in the favour of the complainant and also the accused has stated in his cross examination that the address at which the legal notice was sent has been his residence since last 20 years. Further, as per the Judgment of Hon'ble supreme court in C.C. Alavi Haji v. P. Mohammad (2007) 6 SCC 555, it is not the case of the accused that he was willing to make payment of the cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of the summons and therefore, it is immaterial whether the legal notice was received or not by the accused. Counsel for the accused has also argued that the legal notice is for the demand of Rs.50,00,000/­ and therefore, is not in respect of the cheque in question. However, perusal of the legal notice shows that the loan amount is mentioned to be Rs.50,000/­ only and the demand has been made for total value of the cheque Ex. CW1/A which shows the amount of the cheque is Rs.50,000/­ in para no.3. The complainant has also CC No. 350/13 page no. 5 /11 stated in his cross­examination that the amount in figure i.e. Rs.50,00,000/­ in incorrect which is a typographical error and that the amount in words is correctly written as Rs.50,000/­. This appears to be probable as the legal notice mentions Rs.50,000/­ everywhere else. Hence, the only disputed question to be decided is whether the cheque in question was issued in discharge of legal liability / debt to the complainant. 12 Now, the question of liability of the accused to pay the complainant shall be dealt with. In the present case the complainant has averred that the cheque in question was issued against discharge of liability arising out of loan transaction between the parties. Since the cheque in question is bearing the name of complainant and the present complaint has been filed by the complainant in his own name therefore, presumption arises U/s 118­A & 139 NI Act in favour of the complainant that the cheque was issued in discharge of a legal liability. Thus, the onus to rebut this presumption is on the accused. The accused has disputed his legal liability to make payment on the ground that there was no loan transaction of Rs.50,000/­ between the parties and that the cheque in question was given as security which has been misused by the complainant. 13 It is true that for offence U/s 138 NI Act, the accused is not expected to prove his defense beyond reasonable doubt in order to rebut the statutory presumption. Same has been held by Hon'ble Supreme Court in Kumar Exports v. Sharma Carpets (2009) 2 SCC 513. 14 The complainant in the instant case has failed to disclose his source of income for the alleged loan of Rs.50,000/­ advanced to the accused. The complainant has further stated in his cross examination that he does not have any money lending license and he has not even shown the present loan in his ITR as he does not file the same. Further, the complainant has stated that no written document or loan agreement was executed for the loan transaction. He has also stated that he has not withdrawn the said loan amount from the bank as he had CC No. 350/13 page no. 6 /11 available with him Rs.50,000/­ in cash. The alleged loan of Rs.50,000/­ is evidently in contravention of Section 269 SS of Income Tax Act, 1961 as the loan amount has not been reflected in writing anywhere by the complainant. No written acknowledgement / receipt was also taken from the accused for the loan amount. Hence, it is doubtful whether the complainant had actually given loan of Rs.50,000/­ to the accused. 15 Although the complainant has stated that he knows the accused as the accused used to visit the shop of his son for last 10 years, but he has also admitted that he was not on visiting terms with the accused in his cross­ examination. He has further admitted in his cross examination that he had never visited the house of the accused prior to the date on which he had given the said loan to him. He also did not remember the address of the accused. Thus the complainant has also failed to establish friendly relations with the accused. Further, nothing has been brought on record to substantiate the claim of the complainant of having friendly relations with the accused and advancing friendly loan to the accused and it is indeed very strange that any loan would be advanced to a person who is not a family member, without charging any interest. Infact the complainant has contradicted himself as at one place in his cross­ examination he stated that the accused has not paid any interest till date, while at another place he admits that the accused has paid an interest of Rs.1000/­ per month for two to four months only to the complainant. He has further contradicted himself in his cross­examination wherein at one place he deposed that he handed­over the sum of Rs.50,000/­ to the accused at his home, while at another place he has stated that the accused had come outside his home in the street to take the loan amount. Due to the said contradictions in the testimony of the complainant, the complainant does not inspire confidence of this court as a witness and his testimony is considered to be unreliable. 16 Further, the accused has also stated in his notice U/s 251 Cr. PC, statement U/s 313 Cr. PC and also in his examination in chief that the cheque in CC No. 350/13 page no. 7 /11 question was given to the complainant as security for loan of Rs.30,000/­ taken from the complainant. He has also denied filling in the details on the cheque in question and has only admitted his signatures on the cheque in question. The complainant has stated in his cross­examination that the cheque in question was filled by a friend of the accused who was accompanying him at the time when the cheque in question was handed­over to him. However, complainant has conveniently failed to even mention the name of that friend of the accused who has purportedly filled in the details in the cheque in question. Hence, the fact that the complainant is admitting that certain details in the cheque in question was filled by somebody else, without disclosing even that person's name, probabalizes the defence raised by the accused that the cheque in question was given without mentioning the certain details, because it was meant to be security cheque. 17 The accused has admitted liability of Rs.15,000/­ towards the complainant as he has consistently stated in notice U/s 251 Cr. PC, statement U/s 313 Cr. PC & examination in chief that he had taken loan of Rs.30,000/­ from the complainant, out of which he had returned Rs.15,000/­. Despite the said admission of the accused, yet it does not help the complainant's story much as the accused has admitted the loan amount to the tune of Rs.30,000/­ and not Rs.50,000/­, which is the cheque amount in the present case. Hence, the accused has not admitted his liability with respect to the cheque amount and thus, the accused can rebut the statutory presumption of liability in favour of the complainant on the basis of preponderance of probability. 18 The accused has produced a CD along with certificate u/s 65­B Indian Evidence Act and transcript of the conversation Ex. DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages) to prove that he had taken loan amount of Rs.30,000/­ only from the complainant. However, Ex. DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages) has been objected to by the counsel for the complainant on the ground that it is not proved as per law. CC No. 350/13 page no. 8 /11 Section 65­B (4) states: In any proceedings where it is desired to give a statement in evidence by virtue of this section, a certificate doing any of the following things, that is to say,­ a) identifying the electronic record containing the statement and describing the manner in which it was produced; b) giving such particulars of any device involved in the production of that electronic record as may be appropriate for the purpose of showing that the electronic record was produced by a computer; c) dealing with any of the matters to which the conditions mentioned in sub­section (2) relate, and purporting to be signed by a person occupying a responsible official position in relation to the operation of the relevant device or the management of the relevant activities (whichever is appropriate) shall e evidence of any matter stated in the certificate; and for the purposes of this sub­section it shall be sufficient for a matter to be stated to the best of the knowledge and belief of the person stating it. On bare reading of the above mentioned provision of law, it is clear that the certificate U/s 65­B has to contain averments in respect of any of the sub clauses of Section 65­B (4) and also has to be signed by a person who is in a responsible official position in relation of the operation of the relevant device from which the electronic evidence is produced. In the instant case, the certificate U/s 65­B Indian Evidence Act has been given by the accused himself and he has stated in the said certificate that the data of the memory card of the mobile phone was copied to the computer and then converted and copied on the CD which is part of Ex. DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages). However, the accused has stated in his cross­ examination that the said certificate and copy of the CD has been prepared by his friend Amit @ Sanjay and he has further stated that he has no knowledge of CC No. 350/13 page no. 9 /11 computers. In the instant case the accused has admitted to having no knowledge of computers and also that the said CD has been prepared by his friend, therefore, the certificate U/s 65­B Indian Evidence Act forming part of Ex. DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages) is not a valid certificate as per Section 65­B Indian Evidence Act and hence, Ex. DW1/1 (colly for 16 pages) is inadmissible in evidence. 19 However, even if Ex. DW1/1(colly for 16 pages) is inadmissible in evidence, yet it does not negate the defence of the accused as the accused only has to prove the same according to the standard of preponderance of probabilities. The accused has consistently maintained the same stand in his defence disclosed in notice U/s 251 Cr. PC, in his application U/s 145(2) NI Act, statement U/s 313 Cr. PC and in his evidence. Nothing has come in his cross examination which demolishes his claim that the cheque in question was issued as security for a loan of Rs.30,000/­ only. Further, the testimony of DW­1 is consistent and nothing has been brought in cross examination to show that DW­ 1 is not a credible witness. In these circumstances since the accused is required to prove his defence on the basis of preponderance of probabilities, the defence taken by the accused that the said cheque was issued in blank signed condition stands proved. 20 In view of the above discussion, the complainant has been unable to prove the loan transaction and therefore, there is no question of any legal liability / debt of the accused towards the complainant and the present complaint is liable to be dismissed on this ground alone. Since the complainant has failed to file any document on record to show the liability of the accused, hence, the defence of the accused that the cheque was given in blank signed condition to the complainant and that the same has been misused appears to be probable and credible defence. It is well settled that even though the statutory presumption for offence U/s 138 NI Act is in favour of the complainant, still, the prosecution is burdened with standing on its own legs and has to necessarily prove its case CC No. 350/13 page no. 10 /11 beyond reasonable doubt. In the instant case, there is no written loan agreement, no written receipt, no ITR filed by the complainant even for the period in which the alleged loan transaction took place and cheque in question not filled up by the accused are enough to puncture holes in the complainant's story. Cumulatively, the above circumstances are sufficient to rebut the presumption arising in favour of the complainant U/s 139 NI Act. Accordingly, accused Sh. Vicky Kapoor, S/o Sh. Roshan Lal Kapoor, R/o 131­B, Rani Garden, Delhi­31 stands acquitted of the offence under section 138 NI Act. Accused is directed to furnish bailbond and surety bond in the sum of Rs.10,000/­ U/s 437­A Cr. PC for the next Six month.
