IN THE COURT OF METROPOLITAN MAGISTRATE MUNICIPAL MAGISTRATE­01, KKD, EAST, NEW DELHI Presided by : Ms. Deepti Devesh Complaint No. 430/13 Unique ID No. 02402R0173802013 PS: Krishna Nagar Sukhmal Chand Jain, S/o Late Sh. Bhikkimal Jain, R/o A­35, Gali No.3, Delhi­51 ........Complainant VERSUS Praveen Kumar Jain, S/o Sh. Sukhbir Singh Jain, R/o H.No. 18, Gali No.13, New East Azad Nagar, Near Water Tank, Delhi­51 ........Accused Complaint under section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Ac t Offence complained of : U/s 138 NI Act Date of commission of offence : 12.05.2013 Plea of Accused : Not guilty Final Arguments heard & Concluded on : 14.11.2014 Date of decision of the case : 27.11.2014 Final Order : Acquittal BRIEF FACTS AND REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE: 1 Vide this Judgment I shall finally dispose off this complaint U/s 138 CC No. 430/13 page no. 1 /12 NI Act by complainant Sukhmal Chand Jain against the accused Praveen Kumar Jain. The complainant's story in brief is that that in the first week of December 2012 the accused had approached the complainant for a friendly loan of Rs.90,000/­. On that the complainant gave loan of Rs.90,000/­ to the accused on the basis of friendly relationship with him. In order to repay the loan, the accused issued two cheques i.e. bearing no. 273697 dated 03.01.2013 for Rs.50,000/­ & bearing no. 273695 dated 03.01.2013 for Rs.40,000/­, both are drawn on ICICI Bank, Connaught Place branch in favor of the complainant and had assured the complainant that the same shall be honored on their presentation. The said cheques were presented by the complainant which was returned vide return memo dated 25.03.2013 with endorsement of "Account closed ". Thereafter the complainant issued legal notice dated 22.04.2013. The complainant has also stated that despite service of the legal notice the accused did not make any payment in discharge of his liability. 2 After the filing of the complaint and closing of pre­summoning evidence, vide order dated 04.06.2013, summons were issued to the accused for the offence U/s 138 NI Act. The accused was admitted to bail on 09.09.2013 which was confirmed on 09.10.2013. Thereafter notice U/s 251 Cr. PC was framed upon the accused on 09.09.2013 to which he did not plead guilty and claimed trial. 3 Application U/s 145(2) NI Act was filed by the accused on 09.10.2013, which was allowed vide order dated 29.10.2013. Thereafter the matter was fixed for CE. 4 In order to prove its case, the complainant examined himself as CW­1 who reiterated the contents of the complaint on Oath and filed evidence by way of affidavit. CW­1 exhibited original cheque bearing no. 273697 dated 03.01.2013 as Ex. CW1/A, cheque bearing no. 273695 dated 03.01.2013 as Ex. CW1/B, return memo dated 25.03.2013 as Ex. CW1/C & Ex. CW1/D, legal notice dated CC No. 430/13 page no. 2 /12 22.04.2013 as Ex. CW1/E, Registered post receipt as Ex. CW1/F, net tracking reports as Ex. CW1/G & Ex. CW1/H. During the cross examination of CW­1, he has also filed on record photocopy of his ITR as Ex. CW1/I (Colly). Thereafter CW­ 1 was cross examined and discharged. Complainant evidence closed vide separate statement of the complainant. 5 After that on 13.05.2014 the accused was examined U/s 313 Cr. PC and his statement was duly recorded. All the incriminating evidence were put to the accused, to which accused submitted that he had not taken any loan of Rs.90,000/­ from the complainant and he had given cheques in question to the complainant as security as he had taken loans from the complainant in year 2010 and the cheques in question were given to the complainant in the year 2010 itself. It is further stated by the accused that he had taken loans from the complainant on a number of occasions, which used to be of Rs.20,000/­ that were to be paid by daily installments of Rs.500/­ running into 48 days. He admitted receiving legal notice from the complainant. He further stated that his security cheques have been misused by the complainant. The accused also stated that he wanted to lead defence evidence. 6 Accused examined himself as DW­1. He deposed in his examination in chief that he knows the complainant for last 13­14 years and that he had taken several loans from the complainant beginning from 12.12.2010 till 10.05.2011 and as on 01.11.2010 he was still to pay Rs.32,300/­ as interest to the complainant. He further deposed that he had given four cheques to the complainant, out of which two were blank and two cheques were filled but undated. He further sated that the complainant did not return any of his cheques and that when he took a loan of Rs.20,000/­ from the complainant @ interest of 20% per month, then the complainant asked him to give cheque of double the amount i.e. Rs.40,000/­. He further stated that he has to pay Rs.40,000/­ against the loan of Rs.20,000/­ and he has already paid Rs.19,000/­ to the complainant, when he asked for a further loan of Rs.20,000/­. At that time the complainant asked him to give CC No. 430/13 page no. 3 /12 another cheque of Rs.50,000/­. He also produced the ledger account of complainant maintained by him which is exhibited as Ex. DW1/A and the cash book maintained by the accused which is exhibited as Ex. DW1/B. Thereafter DW­1 was cross examined and discharged, defence evidence was closed and matter was fixed for final arguments. 7 Final arguments were addressed and heard on behalf of both the parties. Ld. Counsel for the complainant has reiterated the averments made in the complaint. He has argued that the accused has failed to disclose any defence. Therefore all ingredients of U/s 138 NI Act stands fulfilled and the accused is liable to be convicted. 8 On the other hand, the Counsel for the accused has argued that the present complaint should be dismissed as there is no legal liability upon the accused to pay the complainant as no loan of Rs.90,000/­ was ever taken by the accused from the complainant. Infact the accused handed over the cheques in question to the complainant as security for loans previously taken from the complainant. He further argued that the accused has already returned a substantial amount of the loan taken from the complainant which is also shown from the entries made in Ex. DW1/B by the accused. 9 Arguments advanced by both the parties have been heard at length. I have also perused the judicial record. 10 For offence U/s 138 NI Act the necessary ingredients to be proved are: a) That the accused had a legal liability / debt towards the complainant. b) That the accused issued a cheque in discharge of the said legal liability / debt. c) That the accused was maintaining a bank account for the cheque issued by him. CC No. 430/13 page no. 4 /12 d) That the cheque was presented by the complainant within its validity period. e) That the cheque was dishonored on his presentation. f) That the complainant issued legal demand notice within 30 days from the date of dishonor of the cheque. g) That the accused failed to pay within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice. If the above ingredients are proved then an offence punishable U/s 138 NI Act is committed. 11 In the present case, the accused has stated in the notice framed U/s 251 Cr. PC that the cheques in question were issued to the complainant as security / advance payment for business transaction along with two other cheques. He also admitted that the details on the cheques in question were filled by him except the date. He has reiterated the same in his statement U/s 313 Cr. PC. He has admitted receiving legal notice from the complainant. Hence, the disputed question to be decided is whether the cheques in question were issued in discharge of legal liability / debt to the complainant. 12 Now, the question of liability of the accused to pay the complainant shall be dealt with. In the present case the complainant has averred that the cheques in question were issued against discharge of liability arising out of loan transaction between the parties. Since the cheques in question are bearing the name of complainant and the present complaint has been filed by the complainant in his own name therefore, presumption arises U/s 118­A & 139 NI Act in favour of the complainant that the cheques were issued in discharge of a legal liability. Thus, the onus to rebut this presumption is on the accused. The accused has disputed his legal liability to make payment on the ground that there was no loan transaction of Rs.90,000/­ between the parties and that the cheques in question were security cheques which have been misused by the complainant. CC No. 430/13 page no. 5 /12 13 It is true that for offence U/s 138 NI Act, the accused is not expected to prove his defense beyond reasonable doubt in order to rebut the statutory presumption. Same has been held by Hon'ble Supreme Court in Kumar Exports v. Sharma Carpets (2009) 2 SCC 513. It is also well settled law that in order to rebut the statutory presumption in favour of the complainant, the accused need not examine himself in evidence or adduce any other defence evidence also. The accused can rebut the statutory presumption on the material already available on record or such cogent evidence can be the cross­examination of the complainant also. The same has been held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Krishna Janardan Bhat v. Dattatraya G. Hegde 2008 Cri.L.J 1172 (SC). Now, it is to be examined if the accused has been able to rebut the statutory presumption U/s 118 & 139 of NI Act on basis of defence evidence or material available on record. 14 The complainant in the instant case has failed to mention the date of the loan amount either in his complaint or evidence by way of affidavit or in his cross examination. He has vaguely stated that the loan was advanced in December, 2012. He has further stated in his cross­examination that the cheques in question were given in the same day as the loan bearing date of January 2013. In the case of G. Veerashyam v. S. Shivashankar & Anr by Hon'ble Andhra Pradesh High Court in Criminal Appeal no. 358/2003 decided on 26.06.2007, it has been held that where the complainant failed to mention even the date of the loan then the fact of advancement of loan is itself doubtful. 15 The complainant has also failed to show his source of income for advancing the loan to the accused. He has stated in his cross­examination that his gross income must be around Rs.2,50,000/­ to Rs.4,00,000/­. He has also placed on record his ITR for the assessment year 2013­14 wherein his gross income is stated to be Rs.2,55,100/­. Furthermore he has furnished his computation for ITR 2013­14 wherein he has shown several loans advanced to different persons including to the accused as on 31.03.2013. The total amounts of CC No. 430/13 page no. 6 /12 loan advanced to all these persons including the accused is stated to be Rs.7,60,000/­. Firstly, the veracity of computation filed along with ITR 2013­14 i.e. Ex. CW1/I (colly) is itself doubtful as the same has not even been stamped and signed by concerned C.A. or even the complainant himself. Secondly, even if the computation is assumed to be true then the complainant has failed to show any source of income for advancing such huge amount of loan totaling Rs.7,60,000/­. Further, it is not the case of the complainant that the alleged loan amount of Rs.90,000/­ has been advanced by way of any written acknowledgement or by way of cheque for which some documentary proof has been brought on record. Infact the complainant has admitted that the loan amount was given in cash. He has also stated that the said cash was available with him and that there is no limit of cash kept by him in his house in his cross­ examination. Such testimony of the complainant whereby his gross income is shown to be limited to approximately Rs.2,50,000/­, and admission that there is no limit of cash kept at his home, makes the complainant's story highly doubtful that the alleged loan transaction ever took place. No loan agreement has also been placed on record by the complainant. 16 The complainant has also admitted in his cross­examination that he has given friendly loans to other persons also. He has also admitted that he has not received any amount back from other persons also. He has further admitted that he has taken cheques from other persons also at the time of giving loan to them. These admissions of the complainant clearly establish that the complainant frequently gives friendly loans and along with it takes cheques from the persons to whom loans are given. The same method has been adopted by the complainant in the instant case also. Since, this is routine practice with the complainant to take cheques at the time of giving loans itself, it appears to be probable that the cheques so taken including from the accused, were taken as security. Further, these admissions also established that the complainant has not received back any amount from any persons and therefore, it again became doubtful as to how the complainant had arranged money to give loan to the CC No. 430/13 page no. 7 /12 present accused also when his previous loans have not been returned to him. 17 Further, if the complainant story is assumed to the true that the cheques in question were given on the day of loan with date of January, 2013 mentioned on them, then also the complainant has offered no reasonable explanation for not depositing the cheque in question on the date mentioned on the cheques in question when they became due and payable to the complainant. He has admitted that he has deposited the cheques in question on 22.03.2013. The complainant has stated in his cross­examination that he had requested the accused to return his money and only after that he deposited the cheque in question. No written demand notice has also been produced by the complainant to show that he had asked the accused to return his money before presenting the cheques in question. Hence, without any reasonable explanation coming from the complainant about whey he waited for almost three months before depositing cheques in question, it appears to be probable that the complainant waited for so long because the cheques in question were meant to be security cheques only. 18 Further, the complainant has stated that the cheques in question were given on the same day as the loan transaction of Rs.90,000/­. Any reasonable person would give only one cheque for repayment of one loan, however, the complainant has claimed that the accused has given two cheques of Rs.50,000/­ & Rs.40,000/­ respectively for repaying the loan of Rs.90,000/­ on the same day. The complainant story also appears to be unbelievable because if the cheques in question were issued on the same day then the accused would have issued the cheques in question in continuous series, but the cheques in question are bearing numbers 273695 & 273697, which are not in continuous series. Further, the accused has stated in his examination in chief that he had earlier issued the cheque for Rs.40,000/­ against a loan of Rs.20,000/­ to the complainant and then later on issued the cheque of Rs.50,000/­ against another loan of Rs.20,000/­ from the complainant. The accused's story appears to be more credible in the face of the fact that the cheques in question are not of CC No. 430/13 page no. 8 /12 continuous series and therefore they must have issued one after the other on different dates as claimed by the accused. 19 The complainant has also admitted taking loans from his sons Neeraj Kumar Jain & Pravesh Kumar Jain which has also been shown by him in his ITR. In the judgment of J. Prakashan v. P.K. Surenderan 2007(4) CCC 713 (SC), the Hon'ble Supreme Court has held that where the complainant shown to be himself borrowing money, his capacity to advance loan is doubtful. 20 From the discussion above it is clear that the material coming on record through cross­examination of the complainant shows that the accused has been able to discharge the burden of rebutting the statutory presumptions in favour of the complainant on the basis of preponderance of probability. Now it is to be examined if the accused has been able to strengthen the rebuttal by adducing evidence in his favour. 21 The accused has stated in his notice framed U/s 251 Cr. PC that the cheques in question were given as security along with two other cheques to the complainant. He has also stated that he did not write the date on the cheques in question while all other details have been filled by him. He has reiterated this in his statement U/s 313 Cr. PC where in he has stated that the cheques in question were given in 2010 for loans taken from the complainant in 2010. He has stated the same under oath in his examination in chief wherein he has stated that he had given cheque in question along with two other cheques to the complainant after filling in all the details on the cheque in question except for the date. Therefore the accused has been consistent throughout the trial in his statements. Further, not only has he been consistent, but he has been diligent in maintaining record of loans taken from the complainant and has provided the exact date of loan with the loan amount in his examination in chief. Infact he has confronted the complainant with the same dates for different loan amounts during his cross­ examination by way of suggestions. On the other hand the complainant, who is CC No. 430/13 page no. 9 /12 the lender in the instant case has not been able to tell any exact date for the loan given to the accused and has also not been able to furnish any written record pertaining to the loan taken in December 2012. This is so, when the complainant being the lender, as a reasonable prudent person should be more interested in keeping record of loans advanced to the accused. Therefore, viewing the statement of the complainant where date of the loan has not been specifically mentioned alongside the statement of the accused where he has given complete details of different loans taken with loan amount on different dates, the statement of the accused appears to be more believable than the complainant's. 22 The accused has also produced his cash book maintained by him showing payment made to the complainant on different dates for loans taken from him as Ex. DW1/B, wherein the relevant entry encircled in red from pages1­ 122. Counsel for the complainant has argued that Ex. DW1/B is not reliable as this is the document prepared by and under the control of the accused and therefore, the accused can easily manipulate the same. He has also stated that during the cross­examination of the accused it has been brought on record that the accused has made wrong entries also which he has corrected by overwriting on the wrong entry and therefore, the same is not reliable. There is no doubt that the document is prepared by the accused himself in his own handwriting but it is no ground for outright dismissal of the evidence adduced by the accused being unreliable because it was under the control of the accused. It is natural that the accused would be the best person aware of the entire facts and circumstances of any given case and therefore is also the best person to adduce evidence in his defence. Counsel for the complainant has not been able to place anything on record to establish that Ex. DW1/B is a manipulated document. Further, perusal of Ex. DW1/B shows that the accused maintained diligent record not only in respect of complainant but in respect of other persons and his other transactions and has been maintaining the same for a long time i.e. since 01.11.2010. It does not appear to be credible that the accused would manipulate a document which contains entries since 2010 while he has been examined and cross­examined in CC No. 430/13 page no. 10 /12 September 2014. Infact Ex. DW1/B predates even filing of the present case which has been filed on 01.06.2013 and therefore, it appears to be incredible that the accused would manipulate or create false records for three years prior to the date of filing of the complaint. The accused has also produced in his defence his ledger account of the complainant maintained by him as Ex. DW1/A wherein he has written about all the loans taken from the complainant since 01.11.2010 on page no.19 at point 'A'. Both the documents ex. DW1/A & Ex. DW1/B corroborates the consistent stands taken by the accused throughout the trial that he has taken loans from the complainant since 2010 and has also made payment to the complainant in small amounts since 2010. 23 Counsel for the complainant has argued that the accused has contradicted himself while putting suggestions to the complainant in his cross­ examination. He has pointed out that in one suggestion he has stated that he had taken loan from the complainant starting from 12.12.2010, while in another suggestion he has stated that he has started repaying the loan taken from the complainant from 02.11.2010. Therefore, it is clear from the contradiction that the accused started returning the loan even before taking the loan and thus the defence of the accused is not tenable. The contradiction pointed out by the counsel for the complainant is a minor contradiction wherein the accused due to long gap between the date of loan and the date of cross­examination of the complainant resulting in natural memory loss could have mistakenly forgotten to suggest loan taken from the complainant in the month of November 2010. Infact he has stated in his cross­examination that he had taken the first loan from the complainant on 01.11.2010 and then starting repaying the loan amount from 02.11.2010. Ex. DW1/A also corroborates the statement of the accused wherein the beginning the loan from the complainant is shown to be on 01.11.2010. Hence, such minor contradiction which is possibly the result of memory loss due to the long gap in time period is not sufficient to discredit entire defence of the accused. CC No. 430/13 page no. 11 /12 24 In view of the above discussion, the complainant has been unable to prove the loan transaction and therefore there is no question of any legal liability / debt of the accused towards the complainant and the present complaint is liable to be dismissed on this ground alone. Since the complainant has failed to file any document on record to show the liability of the accused, hence, the defence of the accused that the cheques were given undated to the complainant and that the same have been misused appears to be probable and credible defence. It is well settled that even though the statutory presumption for offence U/s 138 NI Act is in favour of the complainant, still, the prosecution is burdened with standing on its own legs and has to necessarily prove its case beyond reasonable doubt. In the instant case, there is no written loan agreement, no written receipt, doubt about the capacity of the complainant to advance such loan, no date mentioned for the loan, the cheques not being in continuous series and high probability that the cheques in question were given as security are enough to puncture holes in the complainant's story. Cumulatively, the above circumstances are sufficient to rebut the presumption arising in favour of the complainant U/s 139 NI Act. Accordingly, accused Praveen Kumar Jain, S/o Sh. Sukhbir Singh Jain, R/o H.No. 18, Gali No.13, New East Azad Nagar, Near Water Tank, Delhi­51 stands acquitted of the offence under section 138 NI Act. Accused is directed to furnish bailbond and surety bond in the sum of Rs.10,000/­ U/s 437­A Cr. PC for the next Six month.
