Mayur Vihar M/s Tripti Enterprises, Through its sole proprietor, Sh. Ajay Sood, S/o Sh. Y.P. Sood, Office at 208 First Floor, Patparganj Village, Mayur Vihar Phase­1, Delhi ......... Complainant. Versus 1. M/s Sachit Creations (P) Ltd, 2. Sh. Sachit Chawla, Director of M/s Sachit Creations (P) Ltd, WZ­165/A, First Floor, Opp. Pusa Institute, Village Dasghara, Delhi ......... Accused. COMPLAINT U/s 138 OF THE NEGOTIABLE INSTRUMENTS ACT Offence complained of : U/s 138 N.I. Act Date of commission of offence : 06.01.2013 Plea of Accused : Not guilty Complaint filed on : 05.02.2013 Final Arguments heard & Concluded on : 19.03.2014 Date of decision of the case : 28.03.2014 Final order : Conviction CC No. 308/13 page no. 1 /15 BRIEF FACTS AND REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE 1. By way of the present judgment, this court shall decide the complaint case U/s 138 Negotiable Instrument Act 1881 (as amended up to date) filed by the complainant M/s Tripti Enterprises, through its sole proprietor, Sh. Ajay Sood, against the accused M/s Sachit Creations (P) Ltd and its director Sh. Sachit Chawla. 2. The prosecution story in brief necessary for the disposal of the present case is that, the complainant is a Sole Proprietorship firm engaged in the business of manufacturing and supplying of hosiery knitted fabrics and the accused had approached the complainant for purchase of 2 x 1 RB Lycra, Micro Approx 450 Kg on credit basis and in pursuant to the order of the accused, the complainant supplied the said goods to the accused on credit basis. Hence, the accused had purchased goods worth Rs. 10,28,682/­ from the complainant during the period from 24.03.2012 till 12.09.2012 (though in complaint, affidavit and legal notice it was mentioned by the complainant that he had supplied goods worth Rs.8,49,986/­ but in cross examination complainant clarified that he had supplied goods worth Rs. 10,28,682/­). The copies of relevant bills and invoices whereby goods were sent are on record as Ex. CW1/1(colly) & Ex. CW1/2(colly). The accused had issued a certificate in favour of the complainant certifying that the goods were exclusively for export to Dubai (UAE). To discharge the liability towards the CC No. 308/13 page no. 2 /15 complainant, the accused issued four cheques of Rs.1,00,000/­ each i.e. bearing no.069129, 069130, 069131 & 069132 all are drawn on Axis Bank Ltd, Karol Bagh branch, Delhi out of which the present case has been filed on the basis of cheque bearing no. 069131 dated 26.09.2012 of Rs.1,00,000/­ & bearing no. 069132 dated 26.09.2012 of Rs.1,00,000/­ both are drawn on Axis Bank, Karol Bagh, Delhi branch in favour of the complainant but on presentation the said cheques were returned back dishonored with the remark "INSUFFICIENT FUND" vide dishonour memos dated 27.11.2012. Thereafter a legal notice was sent to the accused on 18.12.2012 by way of regd AD and UPC. It is further alleged that despite service of legal notice accused has not paid the cheques amount to the complainant till the filling of the case. 3. After the complaint was filed, summons were issued against the accused vide order dated 07.02.2013 for the offence U/s 138 Negotiable Instrument Act 1881. On appearance of the accused notice U/s 251 Cr.P.C. dated 01.05.2013 was framed upon the accused to which he pleaded not guilty and claimed trial. 4. In order to prove the case, the complainant examined himself as CW­1 and reiterated the contents of the complaint on oath before this court and filed an affidavit in evidence. He also collectively exhibited certificate issued by the accused in favour of the complainant and original bills as Ex. CW1/1(colly), copies CC No. 308/13 page no. 3 /15 of statement of account as Ex. CW1/2 (Colly), original cheque bearing no. 069131 as Ex. CW1/3, original cheque bearing No. 069132 as Ex. CW1/4, the cheque returning memos as Ex. CW1/5 & Ex. CW1/6, the legal notice of demand dated 18.12.2012 as Ex. CW1/7(colly), The original receipts of registered post and courier are Ex. CW1/7(colly), net tracking report as Ex. CW1/8. Thereafter, the complainant evidence was closed at request. 5. After that on 15.10.2013 the statement of accused was recorded U/s 313 Cr.P.C in which all the incriminating evidence along with exhibited documents were put to the accused. In his statement accused stated that he had not purchased the goods worth Rs.10,28,682/­ from the complainant. It is accepted by him that the document which is on page no.1 of Ex. CW1/1 was issued by him and the same is bearing his signatures, however, the same is in respect of an order which complainant failed to supply on time. It is submitted by the accused that the complainant delayed the delivery by two and half to three months. It is further submitted by him that the four cheques were given by him to the complainant for security as well as payment to be deposited for encashment from time to time. It is further submitted by him that out of these four cheques two cheques were duly honored. He accepted delivery of legal notice. It is further submitted by him that he did not made the payment to the complainant as he was not liable to pay in respect of the CC No. 308/13 page no. 4 /15 two cheques in question, however, he was liable to pay only a sum of Rs.1,00,000/­ to the complainant. It is further submitted by him that the goods supplied by the complainant were of inferior quality and were supplied with delay, due to the same he suffered losses around Rs.12,00,000/­. It is further submitted by him that he had paid a sum of Rs.1,00,000/­ to the complainant by way of RTGS. Thereafter the case was fixed for Defence evidence. However, on 06.12.2013 vide separate statement of the Ld. Defence Counsel the defence evidence was closed and the matter was fixed for final arguments. 6. I have heard Ld. counsels and perused the entire record of the case file and the evidence on record. Both the counsel have referred to a number of cases, I have discussed them at the relevant place. 7. Before proceeding further let us go through the relevant provisions of law. The main ingredient of Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act are as follows:­ (a) The accused issued a cheque on an account maintained by him with a bank. (b) The said cheque has been issued in discharge of any legal debt or other liability. (c) The cheque has been presented to the bank within the period of six months from the date of the cheque or within the period of its validity. (d) When the aforesaid cheque was presented for encashment, CC No. 308/13 page no. 5 /15 the same was returned unpaid/ dishonoured. (e) The Payee of the cheque issued a legal notice of demand within 30 days from the receipt of information by him from the Bank regarding the return of the cheque. (f) The Drawer of the cheque failed to make the payment within 15 days of the receipt of the aforesaid legal notice of demand. If the aforesaid ingredients are satisfied then the drawer of the cheque shall be deemed to have committed an offence punishable u/s 138 Negotiable Instruments Act. 8. Now let us deal with the each ingredient of the section 138 of Negotiable Instruments Act to see whether the case against the accused has been proved or not. 9. In the present matter issuance of cheques in favour of the complainant by the accused is not disputed. It is also accepted by the accused at the time of framing of notice that all the particulars on the cheques in question except date are in his own handwriting. Presentation of the cheques within period of their validity and their dishonor due to the reason 'funds insufficient' vide memo dated 27.11.2000 is also not disputed. Service of legal notice and non payment of cheques amount within 15 days there from is also not disputed. The only disputed question is whether the cheques were given towards discharge of any liability or not. WHETHER THE CHEQUES IN QUESTION HAVE BEEN ISSUED IN DISCHARGE OF ANY LEGAL DEBT OR OTHER LIABILITY: CC No. 308/13 page no. 6 /15 10. It is accepted by the accused that the cheques in question were issued by him in fully filled up condition in favour of the complainant. It is submitted by the complainant that the cheques in question were issued by the accused to pay back his liability towards goods supplied to him. In this situation the presumption of law Under Section 118 (a) & Section 139 NI Act would arise in favour of the complainant. 11. At this stage let us go through the relevant provisions of law. There is a presumption in favour of the complainant u/s 118 (a) Negotiable Instruments Act that until the contrary is proved, it will be presumed that every negotiable instrument was drawn for consideration and every such instrument when it has been accepted, endorsed, negotiated or transferred was accepted, endorsed, negotiated or transferred for consideration. 12. Further Section 139 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 provides that it shall be presumed until the contrary is proved that the holder of the cheque received the cheque of the nature referred in the Section 138 for the discharge in whole or in part of his debt or liability. 13. Now it will have to be examined whether the accused has rebutted the presumption as contemplated by Section 118 (b) and Section 139 of Negotiable Instruments Act. 14. It has been observed by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in V.S. Yadav Vs. Reena 172(2010) DLT 561 that the offence u/s 138 NI Act is a technical offence and the complainant is only CC No. 308/13 page no. 7 /15 supposed to prove that the cheques issued by the respondent were dishonored, his statement that cheques were issued against liability or debt is sufficient proof of the debt or liability and the onus shifts to the respondent / accused to show the circumstances under which the cheques came to be issued and this could be proved by the respondent only by way of evidence and not by leading no evidence. It was further observed that in order to rebut the presumption u/s 139 of the NI Act, the accused, by cogent evidence, has to prove the circumstance under which the cheques were issued. It was for the accused to prove if no loan was taken why he did not write a letter to the complainant for return of the cheques. Unless the accused had proved that he acted like a normal businessman / prudent person entering into a contract he could not have rebutted the presumption u/s 139 NI Act. If no loan was given but cheques were retained he immediately would have protested and asked the cheques to be return and still cheques were not returned he would have served a notice as complainant. Further such cogent evidence can be the cross examination of the complainant or his witnesses (as held in Birender Singh V. state (NCT of Delhi) 2008(1)JCC (NI) 15). 15. Similarly in the present case it was for the accused to prove why he did not write a letter to the complainant for return of the cheques in question. Unless the accused had proved that he acted like a normal businessman / prudent person entering CC No. 308/13 page no. 8 /15 into a transaction he could not have rebutted the presumption u/s 139 NI Act. It was for the accused to have protested and asked the complainant to return the cheques and if still cheques were not returned he would have served a notice as complainant. 16. In the present case there is no cogent evidence whatsoever adduced by the accused to support his plea that the cheques in question were not issued towards any liability. It is held by the Apex Court in Krishan Janardan Bhat v. Dattatrey G. Hegde 2008 that it is for the Court to do a balancing act between the statutory presumption arising in favour of the complainant on one side and the material produced by the accused to rebut such presumption on the other side with the view to ascertain the truth. The following points shows that the defence of the accused is false: a) The present accused had not written any letter to the complainant for returning his cheques. He had also not written any letter to his banker to stop payment of the cheque in question, despite he was aware that his cheques were with the complainant who can misuse the same. Further the accused has not made any complaint to any authority regarding alleged misuse of his cheques by the complainant.(Reliance being placed on Jose Vs. P.C. Joy 2008 (4) RCR (Crl) 251( Kerala HC). b) In the present matter accused has not led any defence evidence. However, in his statement U/s 313 Cr. PC it was submitted by him that the cheques in question along with two CC No. 308/13 page no. 9 /15 other cheques were given by him to the complainant as security as well as for payment. It is submitted by him that out of the four cheques two cheques were duly honored. It is not submitted by him that those two cheques were mistakingly encashed. He has not specified any difference in respect of status of those four cheques. Thus it appears that the present two cheques in question were on similar footing with the two cheques which were encashed. Thus apparently these two cheques were also meant to be encashed. c) It is accepted by the accused that he received the legal notice sent by the complainant, however, it is submitted by him that he could not reply the same. Non reply of the legal notice shows that accused had nothing to say against the legal notice. It shows that he had no objection regarding presentation of the cheques for encashment (Reliance being placed on Rangappa Vs Mohan 2010­TLPRE­0­300 and Iftikar Alam v. Naved Hasan 2012 (1) LRC 172 Delhi). d) The fact that accused has not got the payment of cheques in question stopped, further non replying the legal notice, clearly suggest that all the defences taken by the accused are afterthought. Why the accused kept silent and did not got the payment of the cheques stopped or why not he had written a letter to the complainant stopping them from misusing his cheques. e) The main defence of the accused in the present case CC No. 308/13 page no. 10 /15 is that, the complainant had supplied goods worth Rs.8,49,986/­ to him and he had already made payment of Rs.7,50,000/­ and now he is liable to pay only a sum of Rs.1,00,000/­ and not Rs. 2,00,000/­. It is pointed out by the Ld. Defence Counsel that complainant had written in his legal notice, complaint and affidavit that he had supplied goods worth Rs.8,49,986/­. On the other hand it is clarified by the complainant in his cross examination that he had mistakingly written in his legal notice, complaint and affidavit that he supplied good worth Rs. 8,49,986/­ while infact he had supplied goods worth Rs. 10,28,682/­. To support his contention complainant has relied on the document already filed on record as Ex. CW1/2 which are the summery of the accounts apparently sent by the complainant to the accused (which are not objected by the defence side as no question in respect of these documents was put in the cross examination of CW­1). These two statements clearly shows that till 30.07.2012 complainant has supplied goods worth Rs. 8,49,986/­ to the accused and had received payment of Rs. 5,53,000/­ from him, leaving the balance as Rs.2,96,986/­. The second statement which is dated 01.10.2012 which is also a non disputed document shows that after 30.07.2012 goods worth Rs. 1,12,906/­ (bill no. 4220 date 18.08.2012) & goods worth Rs. 65,790/­ (bill no. 4230 dated 12.09.2012) were supplied to the accused, hence, his total liability became Rs.2,96,986/­ + Rs. 1,12,906/­ + Rs.65,790/­ total amounting to Rs.4,75,682/­. The complainant had taken into consideration payment received by CC No. 308/13 page no. 11 /15 way of two cheques on 25.08.2012 total amounting to Rs. 2,00,000/­. Thus as on 01.10.2012 accused was liable to pay a sum of Rs.2,75,682/­ to the complainant. These calculations prove that the amount Rs.8,49,986/­ was mistakingly written by the complainant while actual sales were infact Rs.10,28,682/­. Further these calculations are also supported by the accompanying bills and delivery challans which are collectively exhibited as Ex. CW1/1. The defence choses not to dispute any such bills. Thus the bills have been duly proved. Hence, it can be said that complainant has proved liability of the accused by positive unchallenged evidence as well, while on the other hand the accused has not brought any document whatsoever on record to prove his defence that he did not receive the goods. f). The Ld. Defence counsel relying on the Judgment passed by Hon'ble Delhi High Court in case titled, M/s Alliance Infrastructure Project (P) Ltd & Anr v. Sanjeev Kapoor Cited as Criminal M.C. No.2225 of 2009 has argued that the case of the complainant is liable to be dismissed as he presented cheques of Rs.2,00,000/­ while he was entitled only for an amount of Rs.1,00,000/­. It has already been proved as un­ objected that accused was liable to pay a sum of Rs.2,75,682/­ to the complainant as on 01.10.2012. Hence, the above mentioned case is of no help to the accused, as facts in the present case are different. 17. Thus the bald plea of the accused having not been CC No. 308/13 page no. 12 /15 substantiated by any cogent, convincing and clear evidence would not be sufficient to rebut the presumption of law. The presumption U/s 118 & 139 of NI Act are mandatory presumptions in favor of the complainant and the same can not be held to be discharged merely by reason of the fact that the explanation offered by the accused is reasonable. It must further be shown that the explanation is a true one. The task of the accused is not as simple as for rebutting the statutory presumption he must placed such material which can inspire confidence of the Court to believe the case of the accused then that of the complainant who is in possession of the dishonored cheques issued by none else but the accused himself (reliance placed on Attar Singh Wadhwa v. NCT of Delhi, Delhi High Court 23.12.2009). A fact is said to be proved when its existence is directly established or when upon the material before it Court finds its existence to be so probable that a reasonable man would act on the supposition that it exists. In the present matter it has been established that the defence taken by th accused is a false one. Since the accused has failed to rebut the presumption this Court can not ask the complainant to prove the liability of the accused by positive evidence as the presumption is living and existing. 18. It has been held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Rangappa Vs Mohan 2010­TLPRE­0­300 (A Three Judges Bench Judgement): CC No. 308/13 page no. 13 /15 We are in agreement with the respondent­ claimant that the presumption mandated by Section 139 of the Act does indeed include the existence of a legally enforceable debt or liability. To that extent, the impugned observations in Krishna Janardhan Bhat (supra) may not be correct. 19. Thus from the above stated discussion it is clear that the accused has failed to rebut the presumptions U/s 118 (b) and 139 of Negotiable Instruments Act. Further when the legal presumption is in favour of the complainant U/s 118 & 139 NI Act he is not required to prove his case independently otherwise there would be no use of the presumption in his favour. The presumptions will live, exists and survive and shall end only when the contrary is proved by the accused, that is, the cheque was not issued for consideration and in discharge of any debt or liability. No doubt a presumption is not in itself evidence, but only makes a prima facie case for a party for whose benefit it exists. However, in the present case with the help of unchallenged documents Ex. CW1/1 (bills and chllans) and Ex. CW1/2 (statement of account) the complainant has been able to positively prove liability of the accused. 20. Considering the entire evidence on record, it stands duly proved that the cheques in question which are CW1/3 & Ex. CW1/4 were issued and drawn in discharge of legal liability of CC No. 308/13 page no. 14 /15 the accused and for consideration. 21. In view of the aforesaid discussion, I am of the considered opinion that the complainant has proved his case against the accused beyond shadow of reasonable doubts. All the ingredients of Section 138 of Negotiable Instruments Act have been duly proved on record. Accordingly, accused M/s Sachit Creations (P) Ltd and it's director Sh. Sachit Chawla, WZ­165/A, First Floor, Opp. Pusa Institute, Village Dasghara stands convicted of the offence U/s 138 Negotiable Instruments Act.
