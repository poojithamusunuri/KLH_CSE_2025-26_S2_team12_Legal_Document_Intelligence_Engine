I have considered the submissions and perused the record carefully. 24. Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act has been enacted to lend credibility to the financial transactions. The main ingredients of the offence under Section 138 Negotiable Instruments Act are: (i) Drawing up of a cheque by the accused towards payment of an amount of money for the discharge in whole or in part of any debt or any other liability (ii) Return of the cheque by the bank as unpaid. (iii) The drawer of the cheque fails to make the payment of the said amount of money within 15 days of the receipt of notice under proviso (b) to Section 138. Explanation appended to the section provides that the "debt or other liability" for the purpose of this Section means a legally enforceable debt or other liability. S. S. Sangwan vs. Rajender Kumar Bansal CC. No. 3116/12 25. Apart from this, Section 139 Negotiable Instruments Act lays down a presumption in favour of the holder of cheque in following terms, "It shall be presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that the holder of a cheque received the cheque, of the nature referred to in section 138, for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any debt or other liability". Also, Section 118 Negotiable Instruments Act states, "Until the contrary is proved, the following presumptions shall be made:-(a) that every negotiable instrument was made or drawn for consideration, and that every such instrument, when it has been accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred, was accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred for consideration;" 26. Thus, the Act clearly lays down presumptions in favour of the complainant with regard to issuance of cheque by accused towards discharge of his liability in favour of complainant. Under the scheme of the Act, the onus if upon the accused to rebut the presumptions in favour of the complainant by raising a probable defence. Such being the legal position, it would be pertinent to refer to the defences raised by the accused to rebut presumptions in favour of the complainant. 27. The first defence raised by the accused is that the cheques in question were misplaced by him on 05.08.2006 qua which he had also lodged complaint with the police. Accused submits that he has never issued cheques in favour of the complainant as alleged. Though accused has raised a plea that <span class="hidden_text" id="span_11"> Page 11 of 15</span> S. S. Sangwan vs. Rajender Kumar Bansal CC. No. 3116/12 cheques in question were misplaced on 05.08.2006 but he has not offered any explanation as to how the complainant came in possession of the cheques. In the entire examination-in-chief, accused is totally silent about the possession of cheques with the complainant. Accused has nowhere stated categorically that complainant had taken his cheques without his knowledge and had misused the same. Even if it is presumed that complainant had somehow acquired possession of cheques in question in the year 2006, it is inexplicable as to why the complainant would present the same in August, 2010 after a lapse of four years and not immediately after getting hold of them in the year 2006. Further, the copy of police complaint brought on record by the accused i.e. Ex. DW1/4 does not bear any date and thus, it is hard to say that the same was lodged in the year 2006 as alleged by the accused. Accused has also not examined any witness on record to prove that he had lodged complaint Ex. DW1/4 with PS Sikandra, Agra. Hence, in absence of any cogent evidence, it cannot be said that cheques in question were misplaced by the accused in the year 2006 as alleged by him. 28. The next defence taken by the accused is with respect to Section 269SS of Income Tax Act. It is submitted that complainant could not have granted loan beyond the sum of Rs. 20,000/- in cash as alleged by him. Now, complainant in his cross-examination has categorically stated that the loan amount was arranged from his savings from agricultural land, <span class="hidden_text" id="span_12"> Page 12 of 15</span> S. S. Sangwan vs. Rajender Kumar Bansal CC. No. 3116/12 pension and his salary. Complainant has also clearly explained that he had retired from Indian Army in the year 1989 and that he has not paid any tax as his income was never taxable. Now, Section 269 SS of Income Tax Act clearly states in its proviso, "...Provided further that the provisions of this Section shall not apply to any loan or deposit where the person from whom the loan or deposit is taken or accepted are both having agricultural income and neither of them has any income chargeable to tax under this Act..." . Thus, since loan amount was arranged from savings of complainant from his pension and agricultural income, the same would fall outside the bar of Section 269SS of Income Tax Act and thus, complainant would not suffer any adverse consequences due to the same. 29. The next defence taken by the accused is that the loan is question was time barred and thus, outside the scope of "legally enforceable debt or other liability" as required under Section 138 of Negotiable Instruments Act. Now, a perusal of the complaint shows that complainant had extended loan of Rs. 4 lakhs to the accused in the month of January, 2006 on different dates. As per complainant, after availing the loan amount, accused left his shop and residence at Delhi and after much efforts, was traced in the month of March, 2010 at Agra. Complainant has submitted that accused had issued post dated cheques of 10.08.2010 towards discharge of his liability. 30. Now, since the loan was granted by the complainant in the month of January, 2006, therefore, statutory period of three <span class="hidden_text" id="span_13"> Page 13 of 15</span> S. S. Sangwan vs. Rajender Kumar Bansal CC. No. 3116/12 years for recovering the loan amount expired much before the cheques in question were issued by the accused. Admittedly, there was no acknowledgment of the debt or part payment by the accused within the statutory period of three years to extend the period of limitation for the purpose of Section 18 or 19 of the Limitation Act. Cheques issued in the year 2010 were clearly outside the period of limitation. 31. In Vijay Polymers Pvt. Ltd. & Anr. v. Vinnay Aggarwal, 162 (2009) DLT 23 and Prajan Kumar Jain v. Ravi Malhotra, MANU/DE/2779/2009, Hon'ble High Court of Delhi had held that cheques issued on a time barred debt would not fall within the definition of 'legally enforceable debt' which is the essential requirement for a complaint under Section 138 of NI Act. It was further held that the extended meaning of debt or liability has been explained in the explanation to the Section which means a legally enforceable debt or liability and that the existence of a legally recoverable debt is not a matter of presumption as laid down by Hon'ble Supreme Court in Krishna Janardhan Bhatt v. D.G. Hegde, AIR 2008 SC 1325 (1). 32. In the instant case, the two cheques which are the subject matter of this complaint were for the discharge of a liability of a loan advanced in the year 2006 which had become time barred when the cheques were issued. This debt is not a legally enforceable debt within the meaning of Section 138 Explanation of the Negotiable Instruments Act. Complainant <span class="hidden_text" id="span_14"> Page 14 of 15</span> S. S. Sangwan vs. Rajender Kumar Bansal CC. No. 3116/12 has failed to establish that the debt in question was legally enforceable. Since the loan is time barred and not legally enforceable, hence, offence under under Section 138 Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 would not be attracted against the accused. 33. Thus, in view of the observations made above, accused stands acquitted of the offence under Section 138 Negotiable Instruments Act. File be consigned to Record Room.
