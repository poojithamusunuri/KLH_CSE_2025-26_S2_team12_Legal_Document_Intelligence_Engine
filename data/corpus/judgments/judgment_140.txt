He has further argued that the complainant has also not led any evidence to prove the alleged transaction. Counsel for the accused has relied upon Hon'ble Supreme Court Judgment Indus Airways Pvt Ltd v. Magnum Aviation Pvt Ltd Criminal Appeal No. 830/2014 decided by Supreme Court on 04.04.2014. 11 Arguments advanced by both the parties have been heard at length. I have also perused the judicial record. 12 For offence U/s 138 NI Act the necessary ingredients to be proved are: a) That the accused had a legal liability / debt towards the complainant. b) That the accused issued a cheque in discharge of the said legal liability / debt. c) That the accused was maintaining a bank account for the cheque issued by him. d) That the cheque was presented by the complainant within its validity period. e) That the cheque was dishonored on his presentation. f) That the complainant issued legal demand notice within 30 days from the date of dishonor of the cheque. g) That the accused failed to pay within 15 days of receipt of legal demand notice. If the above ingredients are proved then an offence punishable U/s 138 NI Act is committed. 13 In the present case the accused has not disputed signatures on the cheques in question and the fact that the same were issued to the complainant. The accused has also not disputed the receipt of the legal notice, although he has stated that he does not understand English and he has stated the same in his reply to the legal notice. However, for offence U/s 138 NI Act, the cause of action arises when the CC No. 32/12 page no. 7 /13 accused fails to make payment within 15 days of receipt of legal notice. Therefore, since the receipt of legal notice is not denied by the accused, hence, it is concluded that cause of action has arisen in favour of the complainant. Therefore, the disputed question to be decided in the instant case is whether the cheques in question were issued for discharge of any legal liability / debt by the accused in favour of the complainant. 14 Now, the question of liability of the accused to pay the complainant company shall be dealt with. In the present case, the complainant has averred that the cheques were issued against discharge of liability for cloth purchased by the accused for which bill no.11214 Ex. CW1/1 has been placed on record. Since the cheques in question are bearing the name of the complainant company, therefore, presumption arises U/s 118­A & 139 NI Act that the cheques were issued in discharge of a legal liability. Therefore, the onus to rebut this presumption is on the accused. The accused has disputed his legal liability to make payment on the ground that he never purchased cloth from the complainant company and that he has never transacted with the complainant company after receiving goods worth Rs.5000/­ in 2009 itself as he did not find goods to be of good quality. 15 It is true that for offence U/s 138 NI Act, the accused is not expected to prove his defense beyond reasonable doubt in order to rebut the statutory presumption. Same has been held by Hon'ble Supreme Court in Kumar Exports v. Sharma Carpets (2009) 2 SCC 513. 16 It is an admitted fact that the parties did not enter into any written agreement for the sale and purchase of cloth. The complainant has not placed on record any written agreement for sale of cloth with the acused. It has been admitted by the accused that he has had only one business transaction with the complainant for goods worth Rs.5000/­ and no written agreement exists even for the admitted transaction with the complainant company. However, the accused has admitted CC No. 32/12 page no. 8 /13 transaction with the complainant only once and that too, with respect to readymade garments only and not cloth. 17 The accused has denied purchasing cloth from the complainant, which is the transaction in question, and has infact stated that the complainant does not deal in sale of cloth but only ready­made garments. The complainant has placed on record bill no.11214 Ex. CW1/1 to prove that the accused had purchased cloth from the complainant. Counsel for the accused has argued that Ex. CW1/1 is only a photocopy and not original of the bill, hence, it is inadmissible in evidence. However, no such objection as to the mode of proof with respect to Ex. CW1/1 has been raised by Counsel for the accused at the time of evidence and hence, the accused can not raise this plea of inadmissibility at this stage. The complainant has not placed any document on record to prove that the complainant is dealing in sale of cloth also as no other bills for sell of cloth have been placed on record, while the accused has placed brochure of the complainant company as Ex. DW1/1 which clearly shows that the complainant company is dealing only in sale of ready­made garments and not cloth. The accused has further placed on record Mark DW1/4 which is the registration certificate of the business of the accused in the name of Paras Traders to show that he is also not dealing with cloth and deals only in ready­ made garments. Thus, the accused could not have placed the purchase order for cloth as claimed by the complainant. 18 Even if it is assumed to be correct, that the complainant company is dealing in both sale of ready­made garments as well as sale of cloth as stated by the complainant, yet the particular transaction in question with the accused is in doubt. On perusal of the bill Ex. CW1/1, it is seen that the said bill does not bear any receiving given by the accused or bears his signature anywhere to show that he acknowledged the receipt of this bill. Even if this is ignored as mere oversight on part of the complainant company, then in the instant case, further, no consignment copy has also been placed on record by the complainant to show that the goods were CC No. 32/12 page no. 9 /13 actually supplied to the accused. Lack of consignment receipt makes the supply of goods itself circumspect. The complainant has also not examined any witness to prove supply of cloth to the accused. It is the case of the complainant that accused himself took the goods from the office of the complainant when he handed over the cheques in question. Even if the story of the complainant is assumed to be true, then no receipt of delivery was taken from the accused and the deposition of CW­1 that accused arranged transport does not inspire confidence. It does not appear to reason that a person without having done any major prior transaction with the complainant company, would come prepared from his shop in Jhansi (U.P.) to complainant's office in Delhi, to take delivery of goods worth Rs.5,00,000/­ even before placing purchase order for goods. Further, DW­1 and DW­2 have stated clearly that accused was not in Delhi on the dates mentioned by complainant. Nothing has come in the cross­examination of DW­2 & DW­3 which discredits their respective testimonies about the accused not being in Delhi on 03.03.2012 and 06.03.2012, the alleged dates when the accused placed purchase order upon the complainant company as well as the alleged dated for preparation of bill number 11223 & 11214. Even though DW­2 is admittedly related to the accused, yet his testimony is considered to be reliable by this court as he has been consistent in his examination in chief as well as in cross­examination. Further, DW­3 is an independent witness and there is no reason to disbelieve his testimony. Hence, the fact that the accused was in Delhi on 03.03.202 & 06.03.2012 itself is in doubt and hence, the entire transaction in question as alleged by the complainant company is also doubtful. This is further substantiated by the fact that it appears to be unbelievable that complainant company would sell goods worth lacks on credit to a new customer. Therefore, the defence taken by the accused that no such transaction ever took place for which bill Ex. CW1/1 seems to be a probable defence on account of lack of any evidence produced by the complainant. 19 The accused has also stated that the cheques in question were given to the complainant as security for further supply of goods in 2009. He has also denied CC No. 32/12 page no. 10 /13 filling in the name of payee and date on the cheques in question and has only admitted his signatures and filling the amount in both figures and words in the cheques in question. CW­1 has stated in his cross­examination that the cheques in question have been filled by the manager of the accused who was accompanying him when he came to the office of the complainant. However, CW­1 has conveniently failed to even name the manager of the accused who has purportedly filled in the details in the cheques in question. Hence, the fact that the complainant is admitting that certain details in the cheques in question were filled by somebody else, without disclosing even that person's name, probabalizes the defence raised by the accused that the cheques in question were given without mentioning the name of payee and date, because they were meant to be security cheques. 20 The said defence also appears to be credible as Ex. DW1/2 (OSR) & Mark DW1/3 clearly shows that the cheques in question were issued in 2009 and one cheque bearing no.005587 was even encashed in 2009, which was stated by the accused in his statement u/s 313, Cr.PC Ex. DW1/2 (OSR) & Mark DW1/3 also probabalizes that the cheques in question, which belong to the same cheque book to which the cheque bearing no.005587 for Rs.5000/­ belonged, which were issued to the complainant in 2009 itself, and the same was used by the complainant only in 2012. Counsel for the complainant has argued that Ex. DW1/2 (OSR) can not be relied upon as it is a document which is in the possession of the accused and therefore can be easily manipulated by him. This submission can not be accepted as there is no law which states that evidence in possession of the accused is, on the face of it, unreliable. It is for the complainant to show unreliability of any defence evidence by way of cross­examination. Nothing is come in the cross­examination which suggests that the evidence adduced by the accused is untrustworthy or unreliable. Counsel for the complainant has also argued that Mark DW1/3 is a photocopy and therefore is inadmissible in evidence. However, it is seen from the record that no such objection as to the mode of the proof had been taken at the time of marking of the document and hence, the objection is deemed to have been waived CC No. 32/12 page no. 11 /13 and can not be raised at this stage. Hence, it is probable that the cheques in question were given for security purposes by the accused in 2009, when he had taken goods worth Rs.5000/­ from the complainant company. It has been held by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in Ravi Kumar D v. State of Delhi & Another Manu / DE / 1538/ 2011 that a cheque issued for security to meet some future liability does not attract Section 138 NI Act. In view of this precedent, since the defence of the accused is proved to the extent that the cheques in question were given as security cheques, hence, there is no legal liability / debt against the accused U/s 138 NI Act. Hence, the presumption u/s 138 NI Act has been rebutted by the accused and the onus has again shifted back on the complainant to prove that the accused was liable towards the complainant company for the cheque amount. It has been argued that if the accused had no liability, then the accused would have issued stop payment instructions as any prudent person and that absence of any such instructions sufficiently disproves the claim of the accused that the cheques in question were issued as security cheques. It is true that the accused should have issued stop payment instructions. However, the return of the cheques in question is not in doubt, and that it is also immaterial for what reason the same were returned, for making out offence u/s 138 NI Act. It is, however, not sufficient to discharge the onus on the complainant as the complainant has not brought sufficient evidence to prove even the transaction in question for which the cheques in question were allegedly issued. 21 Another contention / defense raised by the accused is that the present complaint is not maintainable as it has been filed by an authorized representative who does not have proper authorization to file the present complaint. The complainant has filed Ex. CW1/8 (OSR), which is extracts of the Minutes of Board meeting dated 29.06.2008, to prove authority of the AR to file the present complaint. Ex. CW1/8 (OSR) gives sufficient authority to the AR to file complaints and take other necessary actions with respect to all court cases of the complainant company. All court cases naturally include cases u/s 138 NI Act. There is nothing to CC No. 32/12 page no. 12 /13 bar the AR from filing the present case even if no specific authorization with respect to the cheques in question has been given to the AR. It is well settled that even general authority is sufficient to pursue cases on behalf of companies. 22 From the above discussion, it is established that the complainant has failed to prove the liability of the accused to pay the cheque amount. No written document has been placed on record by the complainant to show that the accused was liable for the cheque amount. It is well settled that the prosecution has to stand on its own legs and has to prove his case beyond reasonable doubts, while the accused only need to puncture holes to create reasonable doubts in the prosecution story. In the present matter, there is no written agreement, no receiving or acknowledgement on the bill dated 03.03.2012, no evidence to prove supply of goods to the accused, plea of alibi of the accused, another person filled the details on the cheques in question and that they were given as security, cumulatively probabalize the defence raised by the accused which is sufficient to rebut the presumption of law arisen in favour of the complainant. Accordingly, accused Sh. Anuruddh Kumar Jain, S/o Sh. Vijay Kumar, R/o 238 Bazar Barua Sagar, Jhansi (U.P.) stands acquitted of the offence under section 138 NI Act. Accused is directed to furnish bailbond and surety bond in the sum of Rs.10,000/­ U/s 437 Cr. PC for the next six months.
