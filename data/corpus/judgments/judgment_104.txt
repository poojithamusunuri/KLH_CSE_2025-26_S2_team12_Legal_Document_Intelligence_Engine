Final Judgment : Acquittal ­ :: JUDGMENT :: ­ BRIEF FACTS AND REASONS FOR DECISION OF THE CASE 1. By way of the present judgment, I shall decide the complaint case U/s 138/142 Negotiable Instrument Act 1881 (as amended up to date) filed by the complainant The Jammu & Kashmir Bank Ltd., through its Branch Manager/ AR Sh. Basharat Syed against the accused Ganga Deen, S/o Late Sh. Sukhdev. Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 2 of 20 2. The facts in brief necessary for the disposal of the present case are that as per the allegations in the complaint, the accused took a personal loan of 3,50,000/­ from the complainant bank and agreed to repay the same with interest in equal monthly installments of Rs.9,000/­. The accused committed default in payment of monthly installments on its due dates and after much persuasions and requests of the complainant bank, the accused for discharge of the said liability issued cheque bearing no. 795861, dated 11.09.2011, for sum of Rs.1,67,000/­, drawn on Punjab National Bank, Maharani Bagh, New Delhi in favour of the complainant. On presentation of the above said cheque through Cheque Truncation System, the same was returned dishonoured with the remarks "FUNDS INSUFFICIENT" vide cheque return memo, dated 16.09.2011. Thereafter, the complainant served the legal notice of demand dated 05.10.2011 to the accused which was sent through Registered Post with AD thereby calling upon the accused to make the payment in lieu of dishonored cheque. It is alleged that accused has failed to pay any sum in response to the legal Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 3 of 20 notice of demand. In consequence thereof the complainant has filed the instant complaint for prosecution of the accused U/s 138 Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. 3. After the complaint was filed, the Branch Manager /Authorized Representative of the complainant bank led his pre­summoning evidence by way of an affidavit and after hearing the AR for the complainant and considering the entire material and documents on record, summons were issued against the accused vide order dated 09.01.2012 for the offence U/s 138 Negotiable Instrument Act, 1881. Thereafter the complainant moved an application for the substitution of the AR for the Bank which was allowed vide order dated 26.05.2012 and Syed Mohammed Shabir Shah was substituted as the new AR for the complainant. On appearance of the accused, a separate notice U/s 251 Cr.P.C, dated 26.05.2012 was given to the accused to which he pleaded not guilty and claimed trial and the matter was fixed for complainant evidence. Accused has placed on record the confirmation letter issued by his department regarding earned leave availed by him as Ex.DW1/1. He admitted that he had Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 6 of 20 issued the cheque in question as a blank cheque with only his signatures but the rest of the particulars were not filled by him. I have heard Ld. counsels for both the parties and perused the entire record of the case file and the evidence on record. In order to bring home the conviction of the accused, the complainant has to show not only unbroken chain of events leading to commission of actual offence on record but also the ingredients of the offence complained of. 10. Before proceeding further let us go through the relevant provisions of law. The main ingredient of Section 138 of the Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 7 of 20 Negotiable Instruments Act are as follows:­ (a) The accused issued a cheque on an account maintained by him with a bank. (b) The said cheque has been issued in discharge of any legal debt or other liability. (c) The cheque has been presented to the bank within the period of six months from the date of the cheque or within the period of its validity. (d) When the aforesaid cheque was presented for encashment, the same was returned unpaid/ dishonoured. (e) The Payee of the cheque issued a legal notice of demand within 30 days from the receipt of information by him from the Bank regarding the return of the cheque. (f) The Drawer of the cheque failed to make the payment within 15 days of the receipt of the aforesaid legal notice of demand. If the aforesaid ingredients are satisfied then the drawer of the cheque shall be deemed to have committed an offence punishable u/s 138 Negotiable Instruments Act. Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 8 of 20 Now let us deal with the each ingredient of the section 138 of Negotiable Instruments Act to see whether the case against the accused has been proved or not. 11.WHETHER THE CHEQUE WAS ISSUED OR NOT The accused has himself admitted to have signed the cheque in question while answering to the question at the time of framing of notice U/s 251 Cr. PC. Further while answering to the question U/s 313 Cr.PC the accused has admitted to have signed the cheque in question and giving the same to the complainant at the time of taking the loan from complainant for purpose of security. Therefore, so far as signing and delivery of the cheque in question by the accused is concerned the same is not disputed. Moreover, in Jaipal Singh Rana Vs. Swaraj Pal 149 (2008) DLT 682 it was held by Delhi High Court that "by putting the amount and the name there is no material alteration on the cheque U/s 87 of the Negotiable Instruments Act. In fact there is no alteration but only adding the amount and the date. It was Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 9 of 20 further observed in the aforesaid judgment that there is no rule of banking business that the name of the payee as well as the amount should be written by the drawer himself. No law provides that in case of cheques the entire body has to be written by the drawer only." In view of the evidence on record it stands proved that the cheque in question was issued by the accused. 12. WHETHER THE CHEQUE WAS PRESENTED WITHIN THE PERIOD OF VALIDITY Perusal of the record reveals that the cheque in question Ex. CW­1/3 is dated 11.09.2011 and it got dishonored vide cheque returning memo which is Ex. CW­1/4, dated 16.09.2011, which is not disputed by the accused clearly shows that the cheque has been presented within period of its validity i.e. within six months from the date of issuance of the cheque. 13.DISHONOUR OF CHEQUE IN QUESTION Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 10 of 20 In the instant case, Syed Mohammad Shabir Shah who has appeared as complainant's witness has got exhibited the cheque, which was issued by him as Ex.CW1/3. He has further got exhibited the cheque returning memo as Ex. CW1/4 to prove the fact of dishonour of the cheque in question. The dishonor of the cheque in question has not been disputed by the accused nor the cheque returning memo has been challenged by the accused. Therefore considering the entire evidence on record it stands duly proved that the cheque in question was dishonored vide cheque returning memo dated 16.09.2011, which is Ex. CW1/4 with the reason "Funds Insufficient". 14.SERVICE OF LEGAL NOTICE OF DEMAND UPON THE ACCUSED In the instant case, Syed Mohammad Shabir Shah, who has appeared as complainant's witness has specifically stated in his examination in chief that the complainant got issued the legal notice of demand dated 05.10.2011, which is Ex. CW1/5 and it was sent to the accused vide Registered Post with AD Card. Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 11 of 20 Postal receipts for the same are exhibited as Ex. CW1/6 and Acknowledgment Cards as Ex. CW1/7. The accused, however, has denied the receipt of legal notice of demand during framing of notice under section 251 CrPC and while answering to the question during his statement U/s 313 r/w 281 Cr.PC. "There is presumption U/s 27 of General Clauses Act in favour of the complainant that properly addressed documents sent by the registered post is deemed to have been delivered if not returned back." I have perused the legal notice of demand it bears the correct address of the accused and the postal receipt issued by the postal authorities in ordinary course is also lying on record. Moreover, the summons were served at the aforesaid address and the accused has put in appearance in compliance thereof. Also, the address furnished by the accused himself on his bail bond is the same address which is mentioned on the legal notice of demand sent to the accused. Even the AD card has been received back duly signed by some person. Considering the evidence on record, I am of the considered opinion that the legal Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 12 of 20 notice of demand was served upon the accused. 15.WHETHER THE CHEQUE IN QUESTION WAS ISSUED IN DISCHARGE OF ANY LEGAL DEBT OR OTHER LIABILITY In the case in hand, Syed Mohammad Shabir Shah who has appeared as complainant's witness has specifically stated in his examination in chief by way of affidavit that the accused had issued the cheque in question in discharge of his legal liability towards repayment of entire loan amount. The accused has, however, deposed in his examination in chief that he had issued cheque in question to the complainant as a blank signed cheque at the time of sanctioning by way of security only. He has also stated in his statement under section 313 CRPC r/w Section 281 CrPC as well as deposed in his examination in chief that he was in his native place in UP, Jaunpur on the date mentioned on the cheque in question. Before deciding this issue let us go through the relevant Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 13 of 20 provisions of law. Section 46 of the Negotiable Instruments Act speaks of the delivery, it reads as follows:­ "The making, acceptance or endorsement of a promissory notice, bill of exchange or cheque is completed by delivery, actual or constructive." Section 118 (b) of the Negotiable Instruments Act provides that until the contrary is proved, the following presumption shall be made. (b) As to date ­ that every Negotiable Instrument bearing a a date was made or drawn on such date. Moreover, there is a presumption in favour of the complainant u/s 118 (a) Negotiable Instruments Act that until the contrary is proved, it will be presumed that every negotiable instrument was drawn for consideration and every such instrument when it has been accepted, endorsed, negotiated or transferred was Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 14 of 20 accepted, endorsed, negotiated or transferred for consideration. Further Section 139 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 provides that it shall be presumed until the contrary is proved that the holder of the cheque received the cheque of the nature referred in the Section 138 for the discharge in whole or in part of his debt or liability. Now the Court shall examine whether the accused is successful in rebutting the presumption as contemplated by Section 118 (b) and Section 139 of Negotiable Instruments Act. To rebut the presumption Section 118 (b) of NI Act, accused has raised the plea of alibi. Accused has taken the defence that he was on earned leave from 10.09.2011 to 15.09.2011 and went to his native place at Jaunpur, and thus had no occasion to issue the cheque in question on 11.09.2011. To support his contention, accused has brought on record the confirmation letter from his office regarding the earned leave availed by him Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 15 of 20 from 10.09.2011 to 15.09.2011 which is Ex. DW1/1. Perusal of Ex. DW1/1 reveals that the accused had written a letter to his department for clarifying the position regarding the earned leave availed by him during the aforesaid period and on the bottom left corner of the same the S.O. (Building) has given his remarks confirming that Sh. Ganga deen was on leave w.e.f. 10.09.2011 till 15.09.2011. Perusal of cross­examination of accused shows that complainant has not cross examied accused on this aspesct. Complainant has not even put any suggestion to the accused. It is no longer Res­intergra that a fact which is not contraverted is deemed to have been admitted. Therefore, complainant has impliedly admitted the fact that accused was at his native place Jaunpur from 10.09.2011 to 15.09.2011. Further Ld. counsel for the accused has also argued that the cheque in question was issued by the accused at the time of sanctioning of loan and not on the date mentioned over the Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 16 of 20 cheque. He has brought to the notice of this court the fact that the date on the cheque in question is 11.09.2011 which is a Sunday and the accused could not have given the cheque to the complainant on that date. Perusal of cross­examination of the AR for the complainant reveals that AR has admitted that the bank is off on sundays. Also, it has never been the case of the complainant that cheque in question was a post dated cheque. So a logical conclusion of this would be that if the bank is closed on sunday, then how can the accused issue the cheque in question to the complainant on 11.09.2011 which was a sunday.. Though the accused has not brought on record any document to show that he was not in town and was in Jaunpur at the relevant time but as the complainant has not even given any suggestion to the accused regarding this fact, the complainant is deemed to have admitted the same. Therefore in the opinion of the court the accused has been successful in raising a doubt that the accused was not in town on the date mentioned on the cheque in Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 17 of 20 question and the cheque in question was not issued on the date mentioned on it. The onus to rebut the presumption under section 138(b) was on the accused which he has succesfully shifted upon the complainant. Now the onus was on the complainant to discharge its burden, however, the complainant has miserably failed to do so . Thus it is evident on record as established by the accused that cheque in question was given to the complainant as blank signed cheque by way of security prior to the sanctioning of loan and was not issued on the date mentioned on the cheque in question. Thus, the accused has clearly rebutted the presumption under section 118 (b) of Negotiable Instruments Act. Therefore, presumption under section 118 (b) does not hold good and cheque was not issued by the accused in discharge of his legal liability. Therefore, Court is of the considered opinion that the Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 18 of 20 complainant has not sufficiently been able to prove its case that the cheque in question was issued by the accused in discharge of his legal debt or liability. 16. THE DRAWER OF THE CHEQUE HAS FAILED TO MAKE THE PAYMENT WITHIN 15 DAYS OF THE RECEIPT OF SAID NOTICE In the instant case Syed Mohammad Shabir Shah who has appeared as complainant's witness has deposed in his examination in chief by way of affidavit that despite service of legal notice of demand accused has failed to pay the cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of legal notice of demand. However, mere non payment of cheque amount within 15 days of receipt of legal notice is not sufficient to fasten any liability U/s 138 Negotiable Instruments Act as it has been established that the cheque was not issued in discharge of the legal liability of the accused. 17. In view of the aforesaid discussion, I am of the considered opinion that the complainant has failed to proved his Case No. 197/12 J & K Bank vs. Ganga Deen 19 of 20 case against the accused. Accordingly, accused Ganga Deen S/o Late Sh. Sukhdev, is hereby acquitted of the offence u/s 138 Negotiable Instruments Act. File be consigned to record room after necessary compliance.
