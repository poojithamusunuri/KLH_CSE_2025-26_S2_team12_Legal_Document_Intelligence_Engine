Before proceeding further it is imperative for me to go through the relevant provisions of law. Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act provides that: "Dishonour of cheque for insufficiency, etc, of funds in the account:Where any cheque drawn by a person on an account maintained by him with a banker for payment of any amount of money to another person from out of that account for the discharge, in while or in part, of any debt or other liability, is returned by the bank unpaid, either because of the amount of money standing to the credit of that account is insufficient to honour the cheque or that it exceeds the amount arranged to be paid from that account by an agreement made with that bank, such person shall be deemed to have committed an offence and shall, without prejudice to any other provisions of this Act, be punished with imprisonment for a term which may be extended to two years, or with fine which may extend to twice the amount of the cheque, or with both: Provided that nothing contained in this section shall apply unless (a) the cheque has been presented to the bank within a period of six months from the date on which it is drawn or within the period of its validity, whichever is earlier; (b) the payee or the holder in due course of the cheque, as the case may be, makes a demand for the payment of the said amount of money by giving a notice in writing, to the drawer of the cheque, within thirty days of the receipt of information by him from the bank regarding the return of the cheque as unpaid; and (c) the drawer of such cheque fails to make the payment of the said amount of money to the payee or, as the case may be, to the holder in due course of the cheque, within fifteen days of the receipt of the said notice. Explanation:For the purposes of this section, "debt or other liability" means a legally enforceable debt or other liability. APPRECIATION OF EVIDENCE IN THE LIGHT OF DEFENCE RAISED BY THE ACCUSED AND THE INGREDIENTS OF SECTION 138 OF THE NI ACT. 8. The issuance of the Cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 to the complainant by the accused has not been disputed. It is also not disputed by the accused that the Cheque in Question Ex. CW1/3 is drawn on the CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 5/13 account maintained by her. Further signature on the cheque in question CW1/3 has also not been disputed by the accused. The presentation, dishonor of the cheque in question and the cheque returning memo has also not been challenged by the accused. Therefore, In light of the evidence on record it stands duly proved that the cheque in question Ex CW1/3 is issued by the accused from her own account and the same is also signed by the accused. Further it also stands duly proved that the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 was dishonored vide cheque returning memo Ex. CW1/4 with the reason "Funds Insufficient". 9. From the perusal of the entire evidence it has come to the fore that two fold defence has been taken by the accused. Firstly, that she has not been served with the legal demand notice by the complainant and Secondly that the Cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 has been issued by her to the complainant in a blank manner only after putting her signatures thereon towards guarantee in respect of the Jewellery taken by her husband and not in discharge of her outstanding liability against bills Ex. CW1/2 dated 23.04.2005 and 24.04.2005 as alleged by the complainant. 10. Coming to the first line of defence taken by the accused that she has not been served with the legal demand notice from the complainant, it is pertinent to note that Sh. Sanjay, who has appeared as complainant's witness CW­1 has specifically stated in his affidavit Ex. CW1/A that the complainant has got issued the legal notice of demand dated 01.02.2006 to the accused which is Ex. CW1/5 and it was sent to the accused on 01.02.2006 itself vide registered Post, receipt of which is Ex. CW1/6. The Complainant has also placed on record the returned AD card Ex. CW1/7. CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 6/13 11. The accused at the time of framing of the notice under section 251 of the Code has admitted the receipt of the legal demand notice Ex. CW1/5. However, surprisingly in her statement under section 313 of the code the accused has denied the receipt of the legal demand Ex. CW1/5. Interestingly, the accused during her cross examination as DW­1 has admitted that in the year 2006 she was residing at H.No. 633, Sector 12, R.K Puram Delhi i.e. the same very address mentioned on the legal demand notice. Further in her evidence as DW­1 the accused also stated that her relative has received the legal demand notice. Further the accused during her cross examination again stated that the returned AD card Ex. CW1/7 bears the signature of her relative. The admission of the accused that the legal demand notice has been received by her relative and the returned AD Card also bears signatures of her relative itself evidences that the legal demand notice was sent on the correct address of the accused. It is trite to say that personal delivery of notice upon the drawer is not a precondition for launching a prosecution u/s 138 of the NI Act. In cases under section 138 NI Act merely sending of notice at correct address is sufficient compliance and not that the notice must be received by the drawer of the cheque personally, otherwise, trickster cheque drawer would avoid receiving the legal demand notice by adopting different strategies and escape from legal consequences of Section 138 of the NI Act. Therefore it is patently clear that the legal demand notice has been served upon the accused. Moreover, In "C.C. Alavi Hazi Vs. Palapetty Mohd. & Anr." Reported in (2007) 6 Supreme Court case 555, it has been held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court that the course open to the drawer where he claims not to have received the notice sent by post but has received copy of the complaint with the summons is that he can within a 15 days of the receipt of summons make payment of the cheque amount and he then cannot contend that there was no proper service of notice, which CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 7/13 admittedly the accused in the instant case has not done. As such non receipt of notice as a defence on part of the accused has no force. 12. Now coming to the second line of defence which has been taken by the accused that the Cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 has been issued by her to the complainant in a blank manner only after putting her signatures thereon towards guarantee in respect of the Jewellery taken by her husband and not in discharge of her outstanding liability towards the bills Ex.CW1/2 as alleged by the complainant. Taking up the first limb of the defence i.e except signatures no other particular in the Cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 has been filled by the accused. It is not disputed by the accused that the Cheque in Question Ex. CW1/3 is drawn on the account maintained by him. Further signature on the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 has also not been disputed by the accused. However, it is also submitted by the accused that the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 was given by her to the complainant towards guarantee in respect of the Jewellery taken by her husband and except signatures no other particulars in the Cheque in Question has been filled by her. During the course of arguments Ld. Counsel for the accused also reiterated the same contentions. Surprisingly except denials and suggestions accused has not brought any evidence on record to support her stand that except signatures she has not filled the other particulars in the cheque in question. Furthermore even believing the stand of accused to be correct still there is also no evidence on record to show that she has not even authorized the complainant to fill the said particulars. More so it is trite to say that by putting the name and date there is no material alteration on the cheque U/s 87 of the NI Act as there is no rule of banking business that the name of the payee as well as the amount should be written by the CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 8/13 drawer himself, as no law provides that in case of cheque the entire body has to be written by the drawer only. Therefore, once the signature on the cheque in question is admitted as has happened in the case in hand, the plea that particulars in the cheque in question has not been filled by the drawer is of no consequence unless some cogent evidence has been lead by the accused that he has not expressly or impliedly authorized the payee to fill the particulars therein. ( Ravi Chopra Vs. State and Another, 2008(2) JCC (NI) 169, Delhi, Jaipal Singh Rana Vs. Swaraj Pal 149 (2008) DLT 682,) Therefore, this defence of the accused also holds no ground 13. Before I advert to the second limb of the defence taken by the accused i.e. the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 has been issued by her towards guarantee in respect of the Jewellery taken by her husband and not in discharge of her outstanding liability outstanding liability towards the bills Ex.CW1/2 dated 23.04.2005 and 24.04.2005, it is imperative for me to go through the provisions of Sections 118(a) and 139 of the Act which read as under: "118. Presumptions as to negotiable instruments.--Until the con­ trary is proved, the following presumptions shall be made­ (a) of consideration.--that every negotiable instrument was made or drawn for consideration, and that every such instrument when it has been accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred, was accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred for consideration;" "139. Presumption in favour of holder.--It shall be presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that the holder of a cheque received the cheque, of the nature referred to in Section 138 for the dis­ charge, in whole or in part, of any debt or other liability. 14. The Division bench of Hon'ble Supreme Court in Krishna Ja­ nardhan Bhat v. Dattatraya G. Hegde, (2008) 4 SCC 54, has held that the accused can discharge the burden of presumption U/s 118 and U/s 139 of the NI Act by raising a probable defence on the strength of "preponder­ ance of probabilities". CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 9/13 15. In RANGAPPA Versus SRI MOHAN AIR 2010 SC 1898 the Hon'ble Supreme Court has held that it is a settled position that when an accused has to rebut the presumption under Section 139, the standard of proof for doing so is that of `preponderance of probabilities'. Therefore, if the accused is able to raise a probable defence which creates doubts about the existence of a legally enforceable debt or liability, the prosecu­ tion can fail. 16. From the reading of the above said provisions and the judg­ ments the position of law which emerges is that once execution of the promissory note is admitted, the presumption under Section 118(a) and Section 139 of the NI Act would arise that it is supported by a considera­ tion. Such a presumption is rebuttable in nature. The accused can prove the non­existence of a consideration by raising a probable defence. If the accused discharges the initial onus of proof by showing that the existence of consideration was improbable or doubtful or the same was illegal, the onus would shift to the Complainant who will be obliged to prove it as a matter of fact and upon its failure to prove would disentitle him to the grant of relief on the basis of the negotiable instrument. The burden upon the accused of proving the non­existence of the consideration can be ei­ ther direct or by bringing on record the preponderance of probabilities by reference to the circumstances upon which he relies. In case, where the accused fails to discharge the initial onus of proof by showing the non­ex­ istence of the consideration, the complainant would invariably be held entitled to the benefit of presumption arising under Section 118(a) and Section 139 of the NI Act in his favour. 17. Now coming to the case in hand, to corroborate her stand the accused has examined herself as DW­1. The accused has admitted that the Cheque in Question Ex. CW1/3 bears her signatures. It is also CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 10/13 admitted by the accused that she and her husband used to have transaction with the complainant. Further the accused has admitted that she had purchased gold Necklace set worth Rs 23,419 from the complainant vide bill dated 24/4/2005 and also admitted that she had purchased Gold Kare, gold Tops (earrings) and ladies diamond ring worth Rs. 30,761 from the complainant vide bill dated 23/4/2005. But the accused has contended that she has made the payment against said purchase i.e bills in question Ex. CW1/2 Collectively dated 23/4/2005 & 24/4/2005. Therefore so far as transaction between the accused and the complainant vide the bills in question Ex. CW1/2 is concerned the same is not disputed. Now coming to the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3, during the cross examination of CW­1 a bill Ex CW­1/X1 (also written as Mark A in examination­in­chief of DW­1) issued by the complainant in the name of the husband of the accused dated 12/02/2005 amounting to Rs 70,080/­ is placed on record by the accused . The accused allegedly contends that the cheque in question has been issued by her as a guarantee for jewellary purchased by her husband from the complainant vide bill Ex CW­1/X1. Interestingly, this stand of the accused itself establishes that the complainant knows the accused as well as her husband and further it is also stands established that the complainant used to sell jewellery to the husband of the accused on credit basis. Further the accused as DW­1 in order to prove that she has made payment to the complainant against the bill Ex. CW1/2 collectively dated 23.04.2005 and 24.04.2005 has also placed on record the bills Ex DW­1/X1 and Ex DW­1/X2. On perusal of the bill Ex DW­1/X1 it is revealed that the same is for sum of Rs. 30,761 and is dated 23/4/2005 and is the original of one bill Ex. CW1/2 dated 23.04.2005 tendered by the complainant. The bills Ex DW­1/X2 is for sum of Rs. 9,950 and is also dated 23/4/2005 but the same is not connected with the present complaint case. From the CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 11/13 mere production of the original bill Ex. DW1/X1 it is not proved that payment has been made by the accused against the said bill. Further the said bills placed on record by the accused also does not proves that the accused has issued the cheque in question for guarantee against the purchase made by her husband vide bill Ex CW­1/X1. Furthermore the accused has even failed to examine her husband in defence for whom she has purportedly issued the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3. Apart from the aforesaid denials and vague defence no corroborative evidence has been lead by the accused to support her stand. 18. It is trite to say that mere denial on the part of the accused would not absolve her from any liability. It was for the accused to prove the circumstances under which the cheque in question was issued by her by leading cogent evidence so as to discharge and rebut the presumption raised against him by the Statute U/s 139 and 118 (a) of the NI Act and mere simplicitor denying the allegations would not be at her advantage. 19. The accused has not brought any material on record and also no cogent explanations or any probable defence which would rebut the shadow of presumption existing in favour of the Complainant. This court do not find any force in the arguments advanced by the learned counsel for the accused that he has succeeded in rebutting the presumption under Section 139 of the Act. 20. From the materials brought on record and evidence lead by the parties It stands duly proved that the accused has failed to rebut the presumptions U/s 118 (a) and section 139 of the NI Act. Therefore, I here­ by hold that the Complainant has proved and substantiated the allega­ tions that the cheque in question Ex. CW1/3 was issued by the accused in CC No. 4459A/14 M/s POPULAR JEWELLERY MART (HUF) Vs SMT. RUBINA THAKUR Page 12/13 discharge of her legal liability and for consideration. Furthermore, ac­ cused has also failed to prove that she has made payment to the com­ plainant within 15 days from the receipt of the legal demand notice. 21. In view of the aforesaid discussion, I hereby hold that the complainant has proved and substantiated its allegation against the accused and all the ingredients of Section 138 of NI Act also stands proved. Accordingly, accused Smt. Rubina Thakur is hereby convicted of the offence u/s 138 NI Act. This judgment contains 13 pages.
